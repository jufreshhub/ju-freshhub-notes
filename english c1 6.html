Comparing the Four Tenses in Context

How to Choose Between Present Simple, Past Simple, Present Perfect, and Past Perfect Inside Paragraphs & Exam Questions

1 The Four Tenses Have Different Jobs

When all four tenses appear together in a paragraph or an exam passage, each tense performs a specific role based on time and meaning:

TenseMain JobExample
Present SimpleRoutine or general factI visit my grandmother every month.
Past SimpleFinished past eventI visited her yesterday.
Present PerfectPast connected to nowI have visited her many times.
Past PerfectEarlier of two past eventsI had visited her before I joined university.
Exam Trick: Never choose a tense solely because you spot one familiar word. Always understand the meaning and time relationship of the whole sentence.

2 How to Recognize Each Tense

A. Present Simple

Ask yourself: Is this a habit, daily routine, or general fact?

Examples:
  • I go to work by car.
  • Ronaldo comes from Portugal.
  • He works in a bank.
Clue Words to Look For:
  • every day
  • usually / always
  • often / sometimes
B. Past Simple

Ask yourself: Did the action finish at a definite time in the past?

Examples:
  • I went to Adama last week.
  • We lived in a small house when I was a child.
  • She visited her friend yesterday.
Clue Words to Look For:
  • yesterday
  • last week / last year
  • two days ago / in 2020
C. Present Perfect

Ask yourself: Is the past experience or action connected to the present?

Examples:
  • We have lived in the same house for 25 years.
  • Have you ever had an operation?
  • What have you done to your lip? (It is bleeding now.)
Clue Words to Look For:
  • ever / never
  • already / just / yet
  • since / for
Important Difference (Past Simple vs. Present Perfect):
• I lived here for five years. → The period is finished (I no longer live here).
• I have lived here for five years. → I still live here now.
D. Past Perfect

Ask yourself: Did one past action happen before another past action?

"I did not go to the cinema because I had seen the film before."

  • 1st Event (Earlier) → Past Perfect: I had seen the film.
  • 2nd Event (Later) → Past Simple: I did not go to the cinema.

3 All Four Tenses in One Story

"Abel lives in Jimma. Last year, he visited Addis Ababa. He has visited several Ethiopian cities in his life. Before he travelled to Addis Ababa, he had visited Hawassa."
SentenceTenseWhy?
Abel lives in Jimma.Present SimpleCurrent permanent fact
He visited Addis Ababa.Past SimpleFinished past event (last year)
He has visited several cities.Present PerfectLife experience up to now
He had visited Hawassa.Past PerfectHappened before another past event

4 Tricky Comparisons — Practice & Analysis

1. "I ______ her since 2008."
A. didn't see    B. haven't seen    C. hadn't seen    D. don't see
Show Answer
✅ B. haven't seen — "Since 2008" indicates a period extending from the past right up to now (Present Perfect).
2. "When I visited her, she ______ bed-ridden for six months."
A. is    B. has been    C. had been    D. was being
Show Answer
✅ C. had been — Her condition had already continued for six months before the past moment when I visited her (Past Perfect).
3. "After Larry ______ the film on TV, he decided to buy the book."
A. sees    B. has seen    C. had seen    D. see
Show Answer
✅ C. had seen — Watching the film happened earlier than deciding to buy the book (Past Perfect).
4. "The boys were playing games when it ______ raining."
A. starts    B. has started    C. started    D. had started
Show Answer
✅ C. started — Past Simple describes the shorter event that interrupted an ongoing past activity (were playing).

5 A Simple 3-Step Exam-Solving Method

Step 1: Find the Time Clue

Look for words like yesterday, since, every day, before, or already.

Step 2: Check Meaning

Is it a routine, finished action, present connection, or earlier past event?

Step 3: Verify Structure
  • • Present Simple: V1 / V1+s
  • • Past Simple: V2
  • • Present Perfect: have/has + V3
  • • Past Perfect: had + V3
