Present Perfect Tense

Sentence Structure, Core Functions, Negatives & Questions, Time Markers, and Present Perfect vs. Simple Past

1 Meaning & Structure of the Present Perfect

The present perfect tense connects a past action directly to the present moment. For example, if you lost your phone yesterday (past action), you still cannot use it today (present result).

Affirmative Formula
Subject + have / has + Past Participle (V3)
She + has + eaten → She has eaten.
Subject & Helping Verb Agreement
I / You / We / They + have
  • I have finished my work.
  • They have eaten lunch.
He / She / It + has
  • He has finished his homework.
  • It has stopped raining.
Past Participle Forms (V3)
Regular (-ed)
  • work → worked
  • play → played
  • finish → finished
Irregular
  • eat → eaten
  • write → written
  • go → gone
  • see → seen

2 Four Main Uses, Negatives & Questions

A. Unspecified Time Before Now

The action happened at some unstated time in the past:

  • I have visited Addis Ababa.
  • She has read that book.
  • They have studied English.
B. Past Action With Present Effect

A past event has a clear result right now:

  • I have lost my keys (I cannot open the door).
  • He has broken his leg (he cannot walk).
  • They have built a road (it is usable now).
C. Recently Completed Action

Finished only a short time ago:

  • Ujulu has cooked dinner.
  • I have just finished my assignment.
  • The teacher has arrived.
D. Life Experiences Up to Now

Experiences you have or have not had in your life:

  • I have never been to Moyale.
  • Have you ever visited Hawassa?
  • She has visited Kenya twice.
Negative Form
Subject + have / has + not + V3

Contractions: haven't / hasn't

  • I have not finished my homework.
  • She has not written the letter yet.
  • They haven't arrived.
Question Form & Short Answers
Have / Has + Subject + V3?

Short answers: Yes, I have. / No, she hasn't.

  • Have you eaten?
  • Has she arrived?
  • Have they finished their work?

3 Present Perfect vs. Simple Past & Time Markers

Present Perfect (Past → NOW)

Focuses on the present result or life experience; exact finished past time is not stated.

Simple Past (Finished Past Time)

Happened at a definite, completed time in the past (e.g., yesterday, last year, in 2015).

Present Perfect (Experience / Result)Simple Past (Definite Past Time)
I have read this novel before.I read this novel yesterday.
She has visited Jimma.She visited Jimma last year.
They have finished their work.They finished their work two hours ago.
Common Present Perfect Time Expressions:
  • 1. Already (sooner than expected):
    Classes have already started.
  • 2. Just (a very short time ago):
    I have just eaten breakfast.
  • 3. Never (at no time in life so far):
    I have never visited Moyale.
  • 4. Ever (at any time — mainly in questions):
    Have you ever travelled by plane?
  • 5. Yet (until now — at the end of negatives & questions):
    She has not written the letter yet.
  • 6. So far / Up to now (total progress until the present):
    We have completed three units so far.
Exam Trap — Never Mix Present Perfect With Finished Past Time!

Do not pair the present perfect with definite finished past-time expressions (yesterday, last year, two days ago, in 2010):

❌ I have read this book last year.
✅ I read this book last year.

4 Graded Practice (Easy → Tricky)

Try filling in each blank first, then tap Show Answer to check the correct verb form and grammar rule.

1. She _________ finished her assignment. (have / has)
Show Answer
✅ She has finished her assignment. (Singular third-person subject "She" takes has.)
2. They _________ eaten lunch. (have / has)
Show Answer
✅ They have eaten lunch. (Plural subject "They" takes have.)
3. I have _________ (do) my homework.
Show Answer
✅ I have done my homework. (Irregular past participle of do: do → did → done.)
4. He has never _________ (travel) outside the country.
Show Answer
✅ He has never travelled (or traveled) outside the country. (Never sits between has and V3.)
5. Tricky We _________ (play) football yesterday.
Show Answer
✅ We played football yesterday. (Watch out! "Yesterday" is a definite finished past time → use Simple Past, NOT "have played"!)
6. Tricky She _________ (just / finish) her assignment, so she can submit it now.
Show Answer
✅ She has just finished her assignment, so she can submit it now. (Recently completed action with a present result → Present Perfect; just goes between has and finished.)
