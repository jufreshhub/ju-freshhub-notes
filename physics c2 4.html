Projectile Motion

Simultaneous Horizontal & Vertical Kinematics, Parabolic Trajectories, Cliff Launches, and Range Optimization

1 What is Projectile Motion?

Our Central Idea: A projectile executes two simultaneous, independent motions—constant-velocity horizontal motion and constant-acceleration vertical motion—occurring over the exact same elapsed time (\(t\)).

Imagine kicking a football at an angle. The ball moves forward horizontally, rises vertically, reaches a maximum height, and falls back toward the ground. A projectile is any object given an initial velocity that subsequently follows a path determined entirely by gravity—such as a stone thrown at an angle, a package released from an airplane, a gunshot, or a javelin.

Exam Fact: Under ideal assumptions, the trajectory of a projectile is strictly a parabola.

2 Assumptions & Standard Sign Convention

  • Gravitational acceleration is constant and directed strictly downward (\(g = 9.8\text{ m/s}^2\), unless a problem specifies \(10\text{ m/s}^2\)).
  • Air resistance and Earth curvature/rotation are negligible.
Horizontally (\(x\)-axis)
$$a_x = 0 \implies v_x = u_x = \text{constant}$$
Vertically (\(y\)-axis, up \(+\))
$$a_y = -g = -9.8\text{ m/s}^2$$
Essential Sign Convention Rule (Upward = Positive)
  • Acceleration: Gravity always pulls downward, so \(a_y = -g = -9.8\text{ m/s}^2\) throughout the entire flight (going up, at the peak, and going down).
  • Vertical Displacement (\(\Delta y = y - y_0\)): If the projectile lands above its launch point, \(\Delta y > 0\). If it lands at the same height, \(\Delta y = 0\). If it lands below (e.g., off a cliff of height \(h\)), \(\Delta y = -h\).
  • Launch Angle: If thrown above the horizontal, \(u_y = +u\sin\theta\). If thrown horizontally, \(\theta = 0^\circ \implies u_y = 0\). If thrown below the horizontal, \(u_y = -u\sin\theta\).
Horizontal MotionVertical Motion
No acceleration (\(a_x = 0\))Constant downward acceleration (\(a_y = -g\))
Horizontal velocity stays constantVertical velocity changes continuously
\(v_x = u\cos\theta\)\(v_y = u\sin\theta - gt\)

3 First Essential Skill: Resolving Initial Velocity

Horizontal:
$$\boxed{u_x = u\cos\theta}$$
Vertical:
$$\boxed{u_y = u\sin\theta}$$
Quick Example (\(u = 20\text{ m/s},\; \theta = 30^\circ\)):
\(u_x = 20\cos 30^\circ = \mathbf{17.32\text{ m/s}}\)  |  \(u_y = 20\sin 30^\circ = \mathbf{10\text{ m/s}}\)
Reconstruction check: \(u = \sqrt{u_x^2 + u_y^2} = \sqrt{17.32^2 + 10^2} = \mathbf{20\text{ m/s}}\)
Critical-Thinking Question 1

If the angle of projection increases from \(30^\circ\) to \(60^\circ\) while initial speed remains \(20\text{ m/s}\):

  • Does horizontal velocity increase or decrease?
  • Does vertical velocity increase or decrease?
  • Which component becomes larger?
Show Answer
• Horizontal velocity decreases (from \(20\cos 30^\circ = 17.32\text{ m/s}\) down to \(20\cos 60^\circ = 10\text{ m/s}\)).
• Vertical velocity increases (from \(20\sin 30^\circ = 10\text{ m/s}\) up to \(20\sin 60^\circ = 17.32\text{ m/s}\)).
• At \(60^\circ\), the vertical component (\(u_y\)) becomes larger (above \(45^\circ\), \(u_y > u_x\)).

4 Fundamental Equations, Peak Analysis & Derivations

A. Horizontal Position
$$\boxed{x = (u\cos\theta)\,t}$$
B. Vertical Position
$$\boxed{\Delta y = (u\sin\theta)\,t - \frac{1}{2}gt^2}$$
C. Horizontal Velocity
$$\boxed{v_x = u\cos\theta}$$
D. Vertical Velocity
$$\boxed{v_y = u\sin\theta - gt}$$
Time-Independent Vertical Relation & Instantaneous Speed
\(v_y^2 = (u\sin\theta)^2 - 2g\Delta y\)   |   Speed: \(v = \sqrt{v_x^2 + v_y^2}\)   |   Angle: \(\phi = \tan^{-1}\!\left(\frac{v_y}{v_x}\right)\)

What Happens at the Highest Point?

Quantity at PeakValue
Vertical velocity (\(v_y\))Zero (\(0\))
Horizontal velocity (\(v_x\))Constant, nonzero (\(u\cos\theta\)) unless vertical launch
Total velocityEqual strictly to horizontal velocity (\(u\cos\theta\))
Acceleration (\(a_y\))Downward, \(9.8\text{ m/s}^2\) (\(a_y = -9.8\text{ m/s}^2\))
SpeedMinimum speed during flight (\(= u\cos\theta\)), not zero
Exam Trap: If an MCQ claims “The velocity of a projectile at its highest point is zero,” that statement is generally FALSE—only its vertical component (\(v_y\)) is zero.
Key Derivations (Same Launch & Landing Height)
1. Time to Peak (\(v_y = 0\)): \(0 = u\sin\theta - gt_{\text{up}} \implies \boxed{t_{\text{up}} = \frac{u\sin\theta}{g}}\)
2. Maximum Height (\(v_y^2 = u_y^2 - 2gy\)): \(0 = u^2\sin^2\theta - 2gH \implies \boxed{H = \frac{u^2\sin^2\theta}{2g}}\)
3. Total Time of Flight (\(\Delta y = 0\)): \(0 = T\!\left(u\sin\theta - \frac{1}{2}gT\right) \implies \boxed{T = \frac{2u\sin\theta}{g}}\)
4. Horizontal Range (\(R = u_x T\)): \(R = u\cos\theta\!\left(\frac{2u\sin\theta}{g}\right) = \frac{u^2(2\sin\theta\cos\theta)}{g} \implies \boxed{R = \frac{u^2\sin 2\theta}{g}}\)

Important Condition: The formulas for \(T\) and \(R\) above apply ONLY when the projectile lands at the exact same vertical height from which it was launched!

Maximum Possible Range

Maximum occurs when \(\sin 2\theta = 1 \implies 2\theta = 90^\circ \implies \mathbf{\theta = 45^\circ}\):

$$R_{\max} = \frac{u^2}{g}$$
Complementary Angles (\(\theta_1 + \theta_2 = 90^\circ\))

Pairs like \(30^\circ \& 60^\circ\), \(20^\circ \& 70^\circ\), or \(40^\circ \& 50^\circ\) yield equal range (since \(\sin 2\theta_1 = \sin 2\theta_2\)), but the steeper angle reaches a higher maximum height and stays airborne longer!

Symmetry Rules & Useful Exam Shortcut (Level Ground)
  • Time Symmetry: Time going up equals time coming down (\(t_{\text{up}} = t_{\text{down}} = \frac{T}{2}\)).
  • Velocity Symmetry: At any equal height on the way up and down, speed is identical, \(v_y\) is equal in magnitude and opposite in sign, and impact speed equals launch speed (\(v_{\text{land}} = u\) at angle \(-\theta\)).
  • Height-to-Range Ratio Shortcut: \(\frac{H}{R} = \frac{\tan\theta}{4}\) (so at \(\theta = 45^\circ\), \(R_{\max} = 4H\)).

Example 1 — Standard Angled Projection (Level Ground)

A football is kicked from ground level with an initial speed of \(u = 20\text{ m/s}\) at an angle \(\theta = 30^\circ\) above the horizontal (\(g = 9.8\text{ m/s}^2\)).

Given: \(u = 20\text{ m/s}\),  \(\theta = 30^\circ\),  \(\Delta y = 0\text{ m}\),  \(g = 9.8\text{ m/s}^2\)
Find: (a) Horizontal & vertical initial velocity components,  (b) Total time of flight (\(T\)),  (c) Maximum height (\(H\)),  (d) Horizontal range (\(R\)).
Step 1 · Resolve initial velocity
\(u_x = u\cos\theta = 20\cos 30^\circ = \mathbf{17.32\text{ m/s}}\)
\(u_y = u\sin\theta = 20\sin 30^\circ = \mathbf{10.0\text{ m/s}}\)
Step 2 · Total time of flight (same level)
\(T = \frac{2u\sin\theta}{g} = \frac{2(20)(0.5)}{9.8} = \frac{20}{9.8} = \mathbf{2.04\text{ s}}\)
Step 3 · Maximum height
\(H = \frac{u^2\sin^2\theta}{2g} = \frac{20^2(0.5)^2}{2(9.8)} = \frac{100}{19.6} = \mathbf{5.10\text{ m}}\)
Step 4 · Horizontal range
\(R = \frac{u^2\sin 2\theta}{g} = \frac{20^2\sin 60^\circ}{9.8} = \frac{400(0.8660)}{9.8} = \mathbf{35.35\text{ m}}\)
Example 2 — Horizontal Projection From a Cliff

A ball is projected horizontally with a speed of \(15\text{ m/s}\) from the top of a cliff \(45\text{ m}\) high (\(g = 9.8\text{ m/s}^2\)).

Given: \(u = 15\text{ m/s}\),  \(\theta = 0^\circ\),  cliff height \(h = 45\text{ m}\) (\(\Delta y = -45\text{ m}\)),  \(g = 9.8\text{ m/s}^2\)
Find: (a) Time of flight (\(t\)),  (b) Horizontal distance from the base (\(x\)),  (c) Vertical velocity at impact (\(v_y\)),  (d) Resultant impact speed (\(v\)) and angle.
Step 1 · Identify initial components
\(u_x = 15\cos 0^\circ = \mathbf{15\text{ m/s}},\quad u_y = 15\sin 0^\circ = \mathbf{0\text{ m/s}},\quad \Delta y = -45\text{ m}\)
Step 2 · Find time of flight using vertical displacement
\(\Delta y = u_y t - \frac{1}{2}gt^2 \implies -45 = 0 - \frac{1}{2}(9.8)t^2 \implies 4.9t^2 = 45\)
\(t^2 = \frac{45}{4.9} = 9.184 \implies \mathbf{t = 3.03\text{ s}}\)
Step 3 · Find horizontal distance from cliff base
\(x = u_x t = 15 \times 3.03 = \mathbf{45.45\text{ m}}\)
Step 4 · Find vertical velocity at impact
\(v_y = u_y - gt = 0 - 9.8(3.03) = \mathbf{-29.7\text{ m/s}}\) (downward)
Step 5 · Resultant impact speed & direction
\(v = \sqrt{v_x^2 + v_y^2} = \sqrt{15^2 + (-29.7)^2} = \sqrt{1107.09} \approx \mathbf{33.3\text{ m/s}}\)
Angle below horizontal: \(\phi = \tan^{-1}\!\left(\frac{29.7}{15}\right) \approx \mathbf{63.2^\circ\text{ below horizontal}}\)
Example 3 — Elevated Angled Projection (Quadratic Solution)

A stone is launched from the edge of a \(20\text{ m}\) high cliff with an initial speed of \(15\text{ m/s}\) at an angle of \(30^\circ\) above the horizontal (\(g = 9.8\text{ m/s}^2\)).

Given: \(u = 15\text{ m/s}\),  \(\theta = 30^\circ\),  cliff height \(h = 20\text{ m}\) (\(\Delta y = -20\text{ m}\)),  \(g = 9.8\text{ m/s}^2\)
Find: (a) Total time of flight (\(t\)),  (b) Horizontal distance from the base (\(x\)),  (c) Speed upon hitting the ground (\(v\)).
Step 1 · Resolve initial velocity
\(u_x = 15\cos 30^\circ = \mathbf{12.99\text{ m/s}},\quad u_y = 15\sin 30^\circ = \mathbf{7.5\text{ m/s}}\)
Step 2 · Set up vertical quadratic equation (\(\Delta y = -20\text{ m}\))
\(-20 = 7.5t - \frac{1}{2}(9.8)t^2 \implies -20 = 7.5t - 4.9t^2 \implies \mathbf{4.9t^2 - 7.5t - 20 = 0}\)
Step 3 · Solve using quadratic formula (\(a = 4.9,\; b = -7.5,\; c = -20\))
\(t = \frac{-(-7.5) \pm \sqrt{(-7.5)^2 - 4(4.9)(-20)}}{2(4.9)} = \frac{7.5 \pm \sqrt{56.25 + 392}}{9.8} = \frac{7.5 \pm 21.17}{9.8}\)
\(t_1 = \frac{28.67}{9.8} = \mathbf{2.93\text{ s}}\)   (reject negative root \(t_2 = -1.39\text{ s}\))
Step 4 · Find horizontal distance & vertical impact velocity
\(x = u_x t = 12.99 \times 2.93 = \mathbf{38.1\text{ m}}\)
\(v_y = u_y - gt = 7.5 - 9.8(2.93) = \mathbf{-21.21\text{ m/s}}\)
Step 5 · Find impact speed
\(v = \sqrt{v_x^2 + v_y^2} = \sqrt{12.99^2 + (-21.21)^2} = \sqrt{618.6} \approx \mathbf{24.9\text{ m/s}}\)

Critical Exam Lesson: Never use \(T = \frac{2u\sin\theta}{g}\) here because the landing height differs from the launch height.

Example 4 — Finding Position & Motion State at a Given Time (Rocket)

A rocket is fired from the ground with initial speed \(u = 100\text{ m/s}\) at an angle \(\theta = 55^\circ\) above the horizontal, and explodes on a mountainside \(t = 12\text{ s}\) later (\(g = 9.8\text{ m/s}^2\)).

Given: \(u = 100\text{ m/s}\),  \(\theta = 55^\circ\),  \(t = 12\text{ s}\),  \(g = 9.8\text{ m/s}^2\)
Find: (a) Coordinates \((x, y)\) of the explosion relative to launch,  (b) Whether the rocket is rising or falling at \(t = 12\text{ s}\).
Step 1 · Horizontal position (\(x\)) at \(t = 12\text{ s}\)
\(x = (u\cos\theta)\,t = 100\cos 55^\circ \times 12 = 57.36 \times 12 = \mathbf{688.3\text{ m}}\)
Step 2 · Vertical position (\(y\)) at \(t = 12\text{ s}\)
\(y = (u\sin\theta)\,t - \frac{1}{2}gt^2 = 100\sin 55^\circ(12) - \frac{1}{2}(9.8)(12^2)\)
\(y = 983.0 - 705.6 = \mathbf{277.4\text{ m}}\)
Explosion coordinates: \((x, y) = \mathbf{(688.3,\; 277.4)\text{ m}}\)
Step 3 · Check vertical velocity (\(v_y\)) to see if rising or falling
\(v_y = u\sin\theta - gt = 100\sin 55^\circ - 9.8(12) = 81.92 - 117.6 = \mathbf{-35.68\text{ m/s}}\)
Since \(v_y < 0\), the rocket is descending (falling) when it hits the mountainside.
Example 5 — Package Dropped From an Airplane

An airplane flies horizontally at a constant speed of \(40\text{ m/s}\) at an altitude of \(100\text{ m}\) when a relief package is released (\(g = 9.8\text{ m/s}^2\)).

Given: \(u_x = 40\text{ m/s}\),  \(u_y = 0\text{ m/s}\),  altitude \(h = 100\text{ m}\) (\(\Delta y = -100\text{ m}\)),  \(g = 9.8\text{ m/s}^2\)
Find: (a) Time taken for the package to reach the ground (\(t\)),  (b) Horizontal distance traveled ahead of the release point (\(x\)).
Step 1 · Initial velocity inherited from plane
\(u_x = \mathbf{40\text{ m/s}},\quad u_y = \mathbf{0\text{ m/s}}\)
Step 2 · Falling time
\(-100 = 0 - \frac{1}{2}(9.8)t^2 \implies 4.9t^2 = 100 \implies t^2 = 20.408 \implies \mathbf{t = 4.52\text{ s}}\)
Step 3 · Horizontal landing distance
\(x = u_x t = 40 \times 4.52 = \mathbf{180.8\text{ m} \approx 181\text{ m}}\)

Critical Question 2: If the airplane moves at \(40\text{ m/s}\), why doesn't the package fall straight down underneath the release spot?

Show Answer

By Newton's First Law (inertia), at the instant of release the package already has a horizontal velocity of \(40\text{ m/s}\). Because no horizontal force acts on it (neglecting air resistance), it keeps moving forward at \(40\text{ m/s}\) while falling—staying directly beneath the airplane the entire time!

Example 6 — Velocity Magnitude & Direction at Any Instant

A projectile is launched from the ground with initial speed \(u = 25\text{ m/s}\) at an angle \(\theta = 53^\circ\) above the horizontal (\(g = 9.8\text{ m/s}^2\)).

Given: \(u = 25\text{ m/s}\),  \(\theta = 53^\circ\),  \(t = 2\text{ s}\),  \(g = 9.8\text{ m/s}^2\)
Find: (a) Horizontal & vertical velocity components at \(t = 2\text{ s}\),  (b) Speed (\(v\)) and direction angle (\(\phi\)) of the projectile at \(t = 2\text{ s}\).
Step 1 · Resolve initial velocity
\(u_x = 25\cos 53^\circ = \mathbf{15.05\text{ m/s}},\quad u_y = 25\sin 53^\circ = \mathbf{19.97\text{ m/s}}\)
Step 2 · Velocity components at \(t = 2\text{ s}\)
\(v_x = u_x = \mathbf{15.05\text{ m/s}}\) (constant)
\(v_y = u_y - gt = 19.97 - 9.8(2) = 19.97 - 19.6 = \mathbf{0.37\text{ m/s}}\)
Step 3 · Speed & direction angle
\(v = \sqrt{v_x^2 + v_y^2} = \sqrt{15.05^2 + 0.37^2} \approx \mathbf{15.05\text{ m/s}}\)
\(\phi = \tan^{-1}\!\left(\frac{v_y}{v_x}\right) = \tan^{-1}\!\left(\frac{0.37}{15.05}\right) \approx \mathbf{1.4^\circ\text{ above horizontal}}\)
Equation of Trajectory & Example 7

Eliminating time \(t = \frac{x}{u\cos\theta}\) inside \(y = (u\sin\theta)\,t - \frac{1}{2}gt^2\) gives the parabolic trajectory equation:

$$\boxed{y = x\tan\theta - \frac{gx^2}{2u^2\cos^2\theta}}$$

Example 7: A ball is launched from ground level at \(u = 20\text{ m/s}\) at an angle \(\theta = 45^\circ\) above the horizontal (\(g = 9.8\text{ m/s}^2\)).

Given: \(u = 20\text{ m/s}\),  \(\theta = 45^\circ\),  \(x = 20\text{ m}\),  \(g = 9.8\text{ m/s}^2\)
Find: The height (\(y\)) of the ball when it has traveled a horizontal distance of \(x = 20\text{ m}\).
Step 1 · Substitute into trajectory equation
\(y = 20\tan 45^\circ - \frac{9.8(20)^2}{2(20)^2\cos^2 45^\circ}\)
Step 2 · Simplify (\(\tan 45^\circ = 1,\; \cos^2 45^\circ = 0.5\))
\(y = 20(1) - \frac{3920}{2(400)(0.5)} = 20 - \frac{3920}{400} = 20 - 9.8\)
\(y = \mathbf{10.2\text{ m}}\)

6 Scaling Laws & Common Examination Traps

Speed Scaling (\(R \propto u^2,\; H \propto u^2,\; T \propto u\))
  • Double initial speed → 4× range & 4× height (2× flight time)
  • Triple initial speed → 9× range & 9× height
  • Halve initial speed → 1/4 range & 1/4 height
Gravity Effect (\(R \propto 1/g,\; H \propto 1/g\))

Both range and maximum height are inversely proportional to gravity: smaller \(g\) yields larger range and height (e.g., on the Moon where \(g \approx g_{\text{Earth}}/6\), jumps go 6× farther and 6× higher).

Critical-Thinking Challenge 3: Comparing \(30^\circ\) and \(60^\circ\) launches at identical speed on level ground: which throw spends more time in the air, and why? Use the time-of-flight equation to prove your answer.
Show Answer
The \(60^\circ\) throw spends more time in the air.
Since \(T = \frac{2u\sin\theta}{g}\) and \(\sin 60^\circ (0.866) > \sin 30^\circ (0.500)\), \(T_{60^\circ} = 1.732\,T_{30^\circ}\).

7 Challenge Problems (With Step-by-Step Solutions)

Challenge 1 — The Unknown Angle: A projectile is launched from level ground at \(30\text{ m/s}\) and lands at the same height \(6\text{ s}\) later (\(g = 9.8\text{ m/s}^2\)). Find its vertical initial velocity, launch angle, horizontal velocity, and horizontal range.
Show Solution
1. \(T = \frac{2u_y}{g} \implies u_y = \frac{gT}{2} = \frac{9.8(6)}{2} = \mathbf{29.4\text{ m/s}}\)
2. \(\sin\theta = \frac{u_y}{u} = \frac{29.4}{30} = 0.98 \implies \theta = \sin^{-1}(0.98) \approx \mathbf{78.5^\circ}\)
3. \(u_x = u\cos\theta = 30\cos 78.5^\circ \approx \mathbf{5.97\text{ m/s}}\)
4. \(R = u_x T = 5.97 \times 6 \approx \mathbf{35.8\text{ m}}\)
Challenge 2 — Cliff Quadratic: Launched from a \(45\text{ m}\) cliff at \(20\text{ m/s}\) at \(30^\circ\) above horizontal (\(g = 9.8\text{ m/s}^2\)). Calculate flight time, range from cliff base, impact speed, and impact angle.
Show Solution
1. \(u_x = 20\cos 30^\circ = 17.32\text{ m/s},\quad u_y = 20\sin 30^\circ = 10\text{ m/s}\)
2. \(-45 = 10t - 4.9t^2 \implies 4.9t^2 - 10t - 45 = 0 \implies t = \frac{10 + \sqrt{100 + 882}}{9.8} \approx \mathbf{4.22\text{ s}}\)
3. \(x = 17.32 \times 4.22 \approx \mathbf{73.1\text{ m}}\)
4. \(v_y = 10 - 9.8(4.22) = -31.34\text{ m/s} \implies v = \sqrt{17.32^2 + (-31.34)^2} \approx \mathbf{35.8\text{ m/s}}\)
5. \(\phi = \tan^{-1}\!\left(\frac{31.34}{17.32}\right) \approx \mathbf{61.1^\circ\text{ below horizontal}}\)
Challenge 3 — Same Range Mystery: Projected at \(25\text{ m/s}\) at \(25^\circ\) above horizontal (\(g = 9.8\text{ m/s}^2\)). Find the complementary angle yielding identical range on level ground and compute that range.
Show Solution
1. Complementary angle: \(\theta_2 = 90^\circ - 25^\circ = \mathbf{65^\circ}\)
2. \(R = \frac{u^2\sin(2\theta)}{g} = \frac{25^2\sin 50^\circ}{9.8} = \frac{625(0.7660)}{9.8} \approx \mathbf{48.9\text{ m}}\)
Challenge 4 — Specified Position: Projected at \(30\text{ m/s}\) at \(40^\circ\) above horizontal (\(g = 9.8\text{ m/s}^2\)). Find its height and vertical velocity when its horizontal distance is \(50\text{ m}\).
Show Solution
1. \(u_x = 30\cos 40^\circ = 22.98\text{ m/s},\quad u_y = 30\sin 40^\circ = 19.28\text{ m/s}\)
2. Time to reach \(x = 50\text{ m}\): \(t = \frac{x}{u_x} = \frac{50}{22.98} = \mathbf{2.176\text{ s}}\)
3. Height: \(y = 19.28(2.176) - 4.9(2.176)^2 = 41.95 - 23.20 = \mathbf{18.75\text{ m}}\)
4. Vertical velocity: \(v_y = 19.28 - 9.8(2.176) = \mathbf{-2.04\text{ m/s}}\) (descending)
Challenge 5 — Planet Gravity Problem: An astronaut on a strange planet jumps a maximum horizontal distance of \(15\text{ m}\) with an initial speed of \(3\text{ m/s}\). Calculate the planet's gravitational acceleration \(g\).
Show Solution
1. Maximum range occurs at \(45^\circ\): \(R_{\max} = \frac{u^2}{g}\)
2. \(g = \frac{u^2}{R_{\max}} = \frac{3^2}{15} = \frac{9}{15} = \mathbf{0.6\text{ m/s}^2}\)
