Scalars and Vectors

Magnitude, Directional Orientation, Vector Algebra, and Speed vs. Velocity

Consider two statements describing a student's walk:

Statement 1

“I walked 5 metres.”

Provides magnitude (size) only.

Statement 2

“I walked 5 metres to the east.”

Adds crucial information: direction.

1 Scalar Quantities

Definition: A scalar quantity is a physical quantity completely specified by a numerical magnitude and its corresponding unit. It possesses magnitude only—no directional component.
Everyday Analogy: If someone asks how much water is inside a bottle, you answer “2 litres.” Saying “2 litres north” makes no physical sense because volume is purely scalar.
Standard Scalar Examples:
  • Mass: \(5\text{ kg}\)
  • Time: \(10\text{ s}\)
  • Volume: \(2\text{ m}^3\)
  • Speed: \(20\text{ m/s}\)
Algebraic Behavior:

Scalars obey the standard laws of ordinary arithmetic:

\(5\text{ kg} + 3\text{ kg} = \mathbf{8\text{ kg}}\)

2 Vector Quantities

Definition: A vector quantity is a physical quantity specified by both magnitude and direction in space.
  • Magnitude: Answers “How much?” or “How big?”
  • Direction: Answers “Which way?”
Standard Vector Examples:
  • Displacement
  • Velocity
  • Acceleration
  • Momentum
Vector Algebra Principle:

Vectors obey vector algebra, not ordinary scalar addition in every situation. Walking \(5\text{ m}\) east and then \(5\text{ m}\) west produces zero net displacement, not \(10\text{ m}\)!

3 Magnitude versus Direction

A Vector Changes Whenever:
  • Its magnitude changes: \(5\text{ m east} \to 10\text{ m east}\)
  • Its direction changes: \(5\text{ m east} \to 5\text{ m north}\)
  • Both magnitude and direction change: \(5\text{ m east} \to 10\text{ m north}\)

4 Speed versus Velocity

Consider a car travelling along a highway at \(60\text{ km/h}\):

Scalar Quantity

Speed

60 km/h

Magnitude only (how fast it moves)

Vector Quantity

Velocity

60 km/h North

Magnitude + specific direction

Exam Rule: Speed and velocity are not interchangeable. Speed is purely scalar; velocity is strictly a vector quantity.
