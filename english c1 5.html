Understanding the Four Tenses

Present Simple, Past Simple, Present Perfect, and Past Perfect — Structures, Core Uses, and Time Relationships

1 First, Understand the Big Picture

Imagine your life is a movie. Each of these four essential tenses plays a distinct role in telling the story:

1. Present Simple

Tells us what normally happens in your movie (habits, routines, facts).

2. Past Simple

Tells us what happened in an earlier, finished scene.

3. Present Perfect

Connects an earlier event or experience directly to your life now.

4. Past Perfect

Tells us what had already happened before another past event.

2 Present Simple — Habits, Facts & Routines

We use the present simple to describe things that happen regularly, general facts, and permanent situations. Think of it as your daily timetable.

Examples:
  • I study English every day.
  • She works in a hospital.
  • The sun rises in the east.
  • Ronaldo comes from Portugal.
Affirmative Structure
I / You / We / They + Base Verb (V1) I play football. / They watch TV.
He / She / It + Verb + -s / -es He plays football. / She watches TV.
Negatives (do not / does not + V1)
  • I do not study at night.
  • She does not study at night.
Questions (Do / Does + S + V1?)
  • Do you study English?
  • Does she study English?

Common time expressions: always, usually, often, sometimes, every day, never.

3 Past Simple — Completed Actions in the Past

We use the past simple for actions or situations that finished completely in the past. Imagine yesterday as a closed chapter.

Positive Formula
Subject + Past Form of Verb (V2)
Examples:
  • I visited Jimma last week.
  • She studied yesterday.
  • We watched a movie last night.
  • They lived in a small house as children.
Regular vs. Irregular Verbs (V2)
Regular (-ed)
  • play → played
  • work → worked
  • watch → watched
  • visit → visited
Irregular
  • go → went
  • eat → ate
  • see → saw
  • write → wrote
Negative: Subject + did not + Base Verb (V1)
  • I did not go.
  • She did not study.
Question: Did + Subject + Base Verb (V1)?
  • Did you visit Addis Ababa?
  • Did she finish her homework?
Common Mistake:
❌ Incorrect: I did not went to school.
✅ Correct: I did not go to school.

Why? Because did already carries the past meaning, so the main verb returns to its base form (V1).

Common time expressions: yesterday, last week, last year, ago, in 2020.

4 Present Perfect — The Past Connected to Now

The present perfect connects a past action or life experience directly with the present moment.

Formula
Subject + have / has + Past Participle (V3)

I / You / We / They → have  |  He / She / It → has

A. Life Experiences

Unspecified time in life:

  • I have visited Hawassa.
  • She has travelled to Kenya.
B. Present Result

Past action affecting now:

  • I have lost my phone.
  • He has broken his leg.
C. Continuing to Now

Started in past, still true:

  • We have lived here for 25 years.
  • I have studied English since 2022.
Negatives & Questions:
  • I have not finished. / She has not arrived.
  • Have you eaten? / Has he completed the assignment?
Key MarkerMeaning & Example
AlreadyEarlier than expected: I have already eaten.
JustA very short time ago: She has just arrived.
NeverNot at any time: I have never travelled abroad.
EverAt any time (questions): Have you ever seen a lion?
YetUntil now (questions/negatives): Have you finished yet?

Common expressions: already, just, ever, never, yet, since, for, so far.

5 Past Perfect — The Earlier Past

The past perfect helps us show which of two past actions happened first. If the teacher started the lesson before you arrived at the classroom, the teacher's action happened earlier → Past Perfect.

Formula
Subject + had + Past Participle (V3)
Examples:
  • The teacher had started the lesson before I arrived.
  • I had eaten before my friends came.
  • She had finished her work when her mother called.
How to Recognize It: "When I arrived, the bus had left."
  • 1st (Earlier Action) → Past Perfect: The bus had left.
  • 2nd (Later Action) → Past Simple: I arrived.

Common expressions: before, after, by the time, already.

6 Comparing All Four Tenses

TenseStructureExample
Present SimpleV1 / V1+sI study every day.
Past SimpleV2I studied yesterday.
Present Perfecthave/has + V3I have studied English.
Past Perfecthad + V3I had studied before the exam.
One Story, Four Tenses (Sara's Story):
  • Present Simple: Sara studies English every day.
  • Past Simple: Sara studied English yesterday.
  • Present Perfect: Sara has studied English for three years.
  • Past Perfect: Sara had studied English before she joined university.

7 Tricky Examples — Test Your Understanding

1. I ______ to Jimma last year.
A. go    B. went    C. have gone    D. had gone
Show Answer
✅ B. went — "Last year" identifies a definite finished time in the past (Past Simple).
2. She ______ her assignment, so she can relax now.
A. finishes    B. finished    C. has finished    D. had finished
Show Answer
✅ C. has finished — The completed past action has a clear result now (Present Perfect).
3. When we reached the cinema, the film ______.
A. starts    B. started    C. has started    D. had started
Show Answer
✅ D. had started — The film started before we reached the cinema (earlier past → Past Perfect).
4. My brother ______ to school every morning.
A. go    B. goes    C. went    D. has gone
Show Answer
✅ B. goes — "Every morning" signals a regular routine, and "my brother" (he) takes -es.
