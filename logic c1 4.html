1 Axiology: The Study of Value (Axios + Logos)

From Greek axios (value/worth) + logos (theory/study), axiology investigates what people and societies consider good, valuable, or preferable (e.g., choosing between telling the truth and losing a friendship vs. lying to protect it). It divides into three major areas:

1. Ethics

Studies moral principles, codes, and rules—determining good/bad, right/wrong, stealing, truth, and whether morality is universal or relative.

2. Aesthetics

Theory of beauty—examining art, enjoyment, perception, taste/sentiment, and whether beauty is objective or subjective.

3. Social/Political Philosophy

Studies civic values—best government & economic systems, justice vs. injustice, state purpose, and obeying laws.

2 Logic: Arguments, Validity, Soundness & Fallacies

Logic studies the principles of right reasoning—like choosing the right highway from Jimma to Addis Ababa so your steps actually reach the right destination. An argument consists of premises (statements providing supporting evidence) and a conclusion (the statement following from them).

Premise 1: All humans are mortal.
Premise 2: Socrates is a human.
Conclusion: Therefore, Socrates is mortal.
A. Valid Argument (Structure Only)

Conclusion follows logically from premises (All birds have wings; a pigeon is a bird → a pigeon has wings). Even false premises can be valid: All fish can fly; tilapia is a fish → tilapia can fly (valid, yet unsound!).

B. Sound Argument (Valid + True Premises)

Requires both valid logical structure AND actually true premises. A valid argument is NOT automatically sound.

Fallacy: A defective reasoning error (e.g., “Everyone in my class believes this, so it must be true”).
