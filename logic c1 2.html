Basic Features of Philosophy

Having vs. Doing Philosophy, Holistic Worldview, Linguistic Clarification, and Perennial Questions

1 Having a Philosophy vs. Doing Philosophy

1. Having a Philosophy (Informal Sense)

Holding personal beliefs or views about life and the universe (“My philosophy is that education is key to success,” “honesty is always important,” or “respect everyone”)—often accepted without deep critical examination.

2. Doing Philosophy (Formal Sense)

Checking the foundation before building upward: actively reflecting on and criticizing deeply held beliefs (asking whether money guarantees happiness, and weighing health, love, justice, and a good life).

Four Qualities of a Philosophical Attitude:
  • Searching: Seeking deeper understanding
  • Critical: Evaluating ideas & arguments
  • Open-minded: Considering differing opinions
  • Tolerant: Listening without prejudice

Requires argumentation & analysis skills—reading books alone is not enough.

Why Philosophers Disagree:
  • Different viewpoints & backgrounds: Varied cultures, education, and experiences (e.g., differing views of a good society/justice).
  • A changing universe: Some embrace change while others favor tradition.
  • Incomplete evidence: Available evidence is interpreted differently.

2 Holistic Worldview & Logical Analysis of Language

3. Rational Attempt to View the World as a Whole (Puzzle Analogy)

Rational means using reason and logic. Imagine a 100-piece puzzle: a scientist examines one piece, an economist studies the economic piece, and an artist studies beauty—whereas a philosopher unites scientific, historical, and religious insights to see how all pieces fit into one consistent worldview.

4. Logical Analysis & Clarification of Concepts

People frequently argue because they use identical words differently. When Student A says “Justice means treating everyone equally” and Student B says “Justice means giving everyone what they deserve,” philosophy clarifies exact meanings—asking whether equality always equals fairness, or whether treating everyone identically can sometimes be unfair.

3 Perennial Problems & Summary Matrix

5. Perennial means enduring across generations. Philosophy explores lasting questions asked throughout history: What is truth? What is life? Why do we exist? What is right and wrong? What is justice? What is beauty? Where does knowledge come from? Is there life after death? Do humans have free will? Attempting answers produced major theories including idealism, realism, pragmatism, existentialism, and analytic philosophy.

Ordinary Factual Question:

“Did Ram make a false statement on his income tax return?”

Answered directly by checking records and evidence.
Philosophical Question:

“What is truth?”

Investigates fundamental meaning and nature.
