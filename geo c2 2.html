Geological Processes — Endogenic and Exogenic Forces

Internal Tectonic/Volcanic Building vs. External Weathering, Erosion, and Landform Modification

1 What are Geological Processes?

Geological processes are the natural activities that change Earth's materials, structures, and landforms over time. Think of Earth's surface as a piece of clay: some forces push, lift, fold, or break the clay (creating new shapes), while other forces wear down, move, and deposit pieces of the clay.

Endogenic Processes:

Internal forces originating inside the Earth that build structural and volcanic landforms.

Exogenic Processes:

External forces acting on existing landforms, modifying, roughening, and lowering them.

2 Endogenic Processes (Internal Forces)

A. Volcanic Activity:

Magma rises from inside the Earth and reaches or approaches the surface. When lava cools and solidifies, it forms volcanic landforms.

B. Tectonic Processes (Crustal Movements & Deformation):
ProcessSimple Meaning
FoldingRock layers bend due to pressure (anticlines = up, synclines = down).
FaultingRocks break and move along fractures (forms block mountains and rift valleys).
OrogenesisMountain building through strong tectonic deformation and elevation.
EpeirogenesisSlow, broad rising or sinking of a large landmass without narrow folding.

3 Exogenic Processes (External Forces)

Exogenic processes act on existing volcanic and structural landforms, modifying, roughening, and lowering them. Four major processes include:

i. Weathering:

Breakdown of rocks in place at or near the Earth's surface.

ii. Mass Transfer:

Downslope movement of weathered materials under gravity (e.g., landslides).

iii. Erosion:

Wearing away and removal of rock/soil by running water and wind.

iv. Deposition:

Accumulation and settling of transported materials in a new location.

4 How Do These Forces Shape Ethiopia and the Horn?

Ethiopia's landmass is the result of the combined effects of endogenic and exogenic processes. Internal movements uplift and structure landforms, while rivers, wind, and weathering sculpt and modify them.

5 Comparison: Endogenic vs. Exogenic & Exam Traps

FeatureEndogenicExogenic
OriginInside EarthExternal / Surface processes
Main RoleBuilds structural and volcanic featuresModifies existing landforms
ExamplesVolcanism, folding, faultingWeathering, erosion, deposition
Other ExamplesOrogenesis, epeirogenesisMass transfer
ResultPlateaus, rift valleys, block mountainsWorn-down and modified landforms
Tricky Exam Distinctions:
  • Weathering vs. Erosion: Weathering breaks rocks down; erosion removes and transports materials.
  • Erosion vs. Deposition: Erosion removes materials; deposition lays them down.
  • Orogenesis vs. Epeirogenesis: Orogenesis builds mountains; epeirogenesis slowly raises or lowers large landmasses.
  • Endogenic vs. Exogenic: Endogenic originates internally; exogenic operates externally.
