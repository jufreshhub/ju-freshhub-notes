Definition of Psychology and Related Concepts

Etymology (\(\Psi\)), Scientific Inquiry, Overt vs. Covert Behavior, and Internal Mental Processes

1 What Is Psychology?

Classroom Scenario:

Imagine sitting in class when a lecturer asks a difficult question:

  • One student smiles.
  • Another looks worried.
  • A third starts sweating.
  • Another remains quiet while thinking deeply about the answer.

Why do people behave, feel, think, and react differently to the exact same situation?

Core Scientific Definition:

Psychology is the scientific study of human behavior and the underlying mental processes. It helps us understand why people behave differently, experience varied emotions, think, remember, and react differently to similar situations.

2 Origin and Meaning of the Word Psychology

Psyche Mind, soul, or spirit
Logos Study, knowledge, or discourse
Psychology (\(\Psi\)) Study of the mind, soul, or spirit
Exam Distinction: The historical meaning of psychology is the study of the mind, soul, or spirit (represented by the Greek letter \(\Psi\), Psi). Its modern scientific definition is broader: studying observable behavior and underlying mental processes.

3 The Three Pillars: Science, Behavior & Mental Processes

A. Science (Empirical Investigation vs. Common-Sense Guessing)

If a classmate is quiet, casual guessing labels them “lazy,” “unfriendly,” or “sad.” In contrast, science in psychology means using systematic scientific methods and empirical data (information obtained through observation, experience, or measurement) to study behavior and mental processes in humans and animals—acting like a detective gathering evidence before concluding.

B. Behavior (Actions and Reactions)

Behavior covers actions and reactions such as walking, talking, laughing, crying, smiling, hand gestures, and facial expressions—divided into two key categories:

1. Overt Behavior (Outward & Observable)

Actions others can directly see or observe (raising a hand, running, laughing).

2. Covert Behavior (Hidden & Internal)

Non-observable internal activities—generally classified as mental processes (thinking about an exam, remembering childhood, feeling nervous inside).

Overt BehaviorCovert Behavior
Outward & directly observableHidden & not directly observable
Talking, running, laughingThinking, remembering, internal feelings

Tricky Example: A student sits completely quietly while furious inside—sitting quietly is observable behavior, whereas feeling angry is an internal mental process.

C. Mental Processes (Internal Activities of the Mind)

Include Thinking (solving math mentally), Feeling (happiness, sadness, fear), Remembering (recalling notes), and Perceiving (interpreting sensory input).
