Modals and Infinitives for Giving Advice

Should / Shouldn't, Ought to, Had Better, Try to, and Direct Base-Form Instructions

1 What Does Giving Advice Mean?

Giving advice means recommending what someone should do in a situation. If a friend says, “I'm failing my English course because I don't study regularly,” you can respond using different levels of strength: “You should study every day” (friendly suggestion), “You ought to make a study timetable” (similar recommendation), or “You had better stop wasting time” (stronger, urgent advice)—as well as infinitives like Try to… or direct verbs like Sit….

2 SHOULD and SHOULD NOT

A. Friendly Suggestion: Subject + should + base verb

Recommends helpful action without forcing (“You should study tonight”). Always use the base verb (✅ should go | ❌ should goes | ❌ should to go).

Positive (should + V1)
  • • I should study.
  • • You should exercise.
  • • He should sleep early.
  • • She should read more.
  • • They should practice English.
Negative (should not + V1)
  • • You should not waste time.
  • • You should not smoke.
  • • Shouldn't write every word.
  • • Shouldn't sleep late before exams.
Question (Should + S + V1?)
  • • Should I study tonight?
  • • Should we leave now?
  • • Should she talk to her teacher?

3 OUGHT TO & OUGHT NOT TO

Structure: Subject + ought to + base verb (Negative: ought not to)

Almost identical in meaning to should (I ought to study harder; You ought to respect your teachers; He ought to exercise regularly; Students ought to attend lectures; Negative: You ought not to waste money; Students ought not to miss exams).

SentenceCorrect?
You ought to study.✅ Correct (ought + to + base verb)
You ought study.❌ Incorrect (missing “to”)
You ought to studying.❌ Incorrect (must use base verb)
You ought to work hard.✅ Correct

4 HAD BETTER & HAD BETTER NOT (Urgent / Stronger Advice)

Structure: Subject + had better (not) + base verb (NO “to”!)

Used when advice is urgent or important (e.g., exam tomorrow while watching TV: “You had better start studying now”; She had better see a doctor; We had better leave early; You had better not waste your time / miss the bus / arrive late).

SentenceCorrect?
You had better go.✅ Correct (had better + base verb)
You had better to go.❌ Incorrect (do NOT use “to”)
You better had go.❌ Incorrect (wrong word order)
You had better not go.✅ Correct (had better + not + base verb)

5 Infinitives, Direct Instructions & Master Matrix

An infinitive is the basic verb form often written with to (to study, to read, to practice, to prepare, to exercise). Advice can also be given directly using TRY TO + base verb (encouraging effort: “Try to prepare yourself in advance,” “Try to study every morning,” “Try to understand before memorizing”) or direct base-form imperatives (“Sit near the front of the room,” “Read your notes regularly,” “Practice speaking English every day”).
