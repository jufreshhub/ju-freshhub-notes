Vocabulary Development & Guessing Meaning from Context

Context Clues, Scanning vs. Skimming, Academic Reading Vocabulary, and Sentence Analysis

Have you ever read an English sentence and found one word you didn't understand? You don't always need a dictionary. Often, the words around the difficult word give you clues about its meaning.

This essential reading skill is called guessing meaning from context.

1 What is Context?

Context means the surrounding words, sentences, or situation that help us understand an unfamiliar word.

Example Sentence:

"Although the road was narrow, the driver managed to pass through it."

Even if you don't know the word narrow, the sentence gives a clue: it was difficult to pass through because it was not wide. Therefore, narrow = not wide.

Think of Context Like a Detective:
  • The unknown word is the mystery.
  • The surrounding sentence gives you clues.
  • You combine the clues to discover the meaning.

2 Four Ways to Guess Meaning from Context

A. Look for Examples

Signal words like such as, for example, including introduce helpful examples.

"Fruits such as mangoes, oranges, and bananas are nutritious."

B. Look for Contrast

Words like but, however, although, unlike show opposite meanings.

"Unlike his talkative brother, Dawit is quiet." → (talkative = talks a lot)

C. Look for Explanation / Definition

Sometimes the sentence directly defines the difficult word.

"An herbivore is an animal that feeds mainly on plants."

D. Look at the Whole Situation

Use cause-and-effect or common sense from the whole scenario.

"After walking for five hours without water, the hikers were exhausted." → (extremely tired)

Exam Tip: Never guess from only one isolated word. Always read the whole sentence—and sometimes the sentence before or after it.

Scan vs. Skim — Don't Confuse Them!

SCAN (Specific Detail)

Search quickly for one specific detail, such as a name, date, keyword, or number.

SKIM (General Idea)

Read quickly across headings and topic sentences to understand the overall gist or general idea.

Easy Memory Trick:
  • Scan = Search for something specific (dates, names, numbers).
  • Skim = See the general idea (main point of a passage).

Understanding the Words in Real Sentences:
1. Keys (Figurative)

"Reading is one of the keys that unlock the door to a career."

Here, keys means something that helps you achieve success—not a metal key.

2. At their disposal

"Students have many reading skills at their disposal."

Means students have many reading skills available to use.

3. Extract

"Students read to extract essential knowledge from textbooks."

Means to obtain or take out useful information.

4. Glancing through

"She was glancing through a magazine."

Means she was looking through it quickly, not reading every word carefully.

Three Key Ideas to Remember:
  • Context clues help you discover the meaning of unknown words without stopping for a dictionary.
  • Scan for specific information; skim for the general idea.
  • Learn vocabulary by understanding how words work inside real sentences, not only by memorizing isolated definitions.
