Active Voice

Understanding Active Sentence Construction, Subject-Verb-Object Structure, Intransitive Active Verbs, and Forming Active Sentences Across Tenses

1 What is Active Voice?

Definition:

Active voice is a sentence construction in which the subject performs the action expressed by the verb.

Imagine a student cleaning a classroom. The student is doing the action:

"The student cleans the classroom."

Subject The student
Verb cleans
Object the classroom

2 The Basic Structure (Subject + Verb + Object)

Subject (Who acts?) Farmers
Verb (Action) grow
Object (Receives) crops
→ "Farmers grow crops." (Active Voice)
Remember: Not Every Active Sentence Needs an Object!

Intransitive verbs also form active sentences because the subject still performs the action:

  • Birds fly.
  • Babies cry.
  • The population increases.

3 Identifying Active Voice

The easiest way to recognize active voice is to ask: "Who is doing the action?" Notice that in active sentences, the doer comes before the verb:

Active SentenceWho Performs the Action?
Sara writes a letter.Sara
The teacher explains the lesson.The teacher
Scientists study population.Scientists
The government builds roads.The government
Three Steps to Check Any Sentence:
  1. Find the verb (action).
  2. Ask: "Who or what performs this action?"
  3. If the grammatical subject performs the action, the sentence is active.
Example 1:

"The students clean the classroom."

Verb: clean → Who cleans? The students → Active.

Example 2 (Academic):

"People who study population have developed a special kind of bar graph."

Verb: have developed → Who developed it? People who study population → Active.

4 Forming Active Sentences Across Tenses

3-Step Construction Process:
  • Step 1: Choose who performs the action (The doctor).
  • Step 2: Choose the correct verb tense (examines).
  • Step 3: Add the object if the verb needs one (the patient).

→ "The doctor examines the patient."

TenseActive Sentence
Present SimpleThe teacher teaches English.
Past SimpleThe teacher taught English.
Future SimpleThe teacher will teach English.
Present PerfectThe teacher has taught English.
Easy Way to Remember: Active = The Subject Does It!
Important Exam Trick: Never assume a sentence is active just because it starts with a person or a noun (e.g., "The baby was carried" starts with a person, yet it is passive). Always check whether the subject actually performs the action!
