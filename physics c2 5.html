Vector Representation

Algebraic Symbols, Geometric Arrow Diagrams, Parallel Transport, and Scalar Multiplication

1 Algebraic Method of Representation

In mathematical physics, two primary frameworks are used to represent vectors: the algebraic method and the geometric method. The algebraic method denotes a vector using a letter or symbol topped with a directional arrow:

$$\vec{A}, \qquad \vec{B}, \qquad \vec{V}$$
\(\vec{A}\)

The complete vector (magnitude + direction)

\(|\vec{A}|\)

The magnitude of \(\vec{A}\) (positive scalar)

\(A\)

Alternative scalar notation for magnitude

Algebraic Example:

A displacement vector of \(5\text{ m}\) pointing east is written as:

\(\vec{A} = 5\text{ m east} \qquad \text{while its magnitude alone is} \qquad |\vec{A}| = A = 5\text{ m}\)

2 Geometric Method & Fundamental Arrow Rules

The geometric method visually represents a vector as a directed straight line segment (arrow) drawn to a chosen scale.

Rule 3 (Parallel Transport): Imagine drawing an arrow on a transparent sheet and sliding it across a table without rotating it or stretching its length. The relocated arrow represents the exact same vector because neither its magnitude nor its direction changed.

3 When Does a Vector Change?

Unlike parallel translation, a vector changes whenever its magnitude changes, its direction changes, or both change simultaneously:

Initial Vector Modified Vector What Changed?
\(5\text{ m east}\) \(10\text{ m east}\) Magnitude only
\(5\text{ m east}\) \(5\text{ m north}\) Direction only
\(5\text{ m east}\) \(10\text{ m north}\) Both magnitude and direction

4 Multiplying a Vector by Scalars

A. Multiplication by a Pure Real Number

Multiplying \(\vec{A} = 3\text{ m east}\) by a positive number scales its magnitude without changing direction. Multiplying by a negative number scales its magnitude and reverses its direction (\(180^\circ\) flip):

B. Multiplication by a Scalar Physical Quantity

Multiplying a vector by a physical scalar produces a new vector quantity with combined units:

$$\text{Velocity} \times \text{Time} = \text{Displacement} \qquad \left(\frac{\text{m}}{\cancel{\text{s}}} \times \cancel{\text{s}} = \text{m}\right)$$
