The Concept of Force as a Measure of Interaction

Fundamental Interactions, Characterization Criteria, Net Force Vectors, and Equilibrium

This is the foundation of dynamics. In kinematics, we described how objects move. Now we begin asking a deeper question:

What causes an object to change its motion?

1 What is Force?

Definition: Force is any interaction that changes, or tends to change, the motion of an object.
A Force Can:
  • Move an object or tend to move it.
  • Stop an object or tend to stop its motion.
  • Change the direction of motion.
  • Change the shape or size of a body.
Simple Analogy (Football):
  • Kick it → begins moving.
  • Catch it → stops moving.
  • Kick it sideways → direction changes.
  • Squeeze a soft ball → shape changes.

Important: A force does not always produce motion. For example, pushing against a heavy concrete wall without moving it still involves applying a force.

2 Force as a Measure of Interaction (Four Fundamental Forces)

All physical particles in the universe interact through four fundamental forces:

1. Gravitational Force

The universal attractive interaction associated with gravity (e.g., Earth attracting objects toward its center).

2. Electromagnetic Force

The interaction involving electric charges and magnetic effects.

3. Strong Nuclear Force

A fundamental short-range interaction responsible for binding protons and neutrons together inside the atomic nucleus.

4. Weak Nuclear Force

A fundamental interaction governing certain subatomic processes such as radioactive beta decay.

Fundamental Principle: All other forces in nature trace back to these four fundamental interactions. For example, friction and the normal force are not separate fundamental forces—they arise from electromagnetic interactions between surface atoms and molecules.

1. Particles Affected

Which types of particles experience that particular interaction?

2. Relative Strength

How strong is the interaction compared with the other fundamental forces?

3. Range

Over what spatial distance is the interaction effective?

4. Mediating Particles

4 Net Force (\(\vec{F}_{\text{net}}\))

Definition:

Net force is the vector sum of all individual forces acting simultaneously on an object:

$$\boxed{\vec{F}_{\text{net}} = \sum \vec{F}}$$

An object accelerates only if the net force acting on it is non-zero.

1. Same Direction

\(10\text{ N}\) right + \(15\text{ N}\) right:

\(F_{\text{net}} = 25\text{ N right}\)

2. Opposite Directions

\(20\text{ N}\) right + \(8\text{ N}\) left:

\(F_{\text{net}} = 12\text{ N right}\)

3. Balanced Forces

Book on table (gravity = normal):

\(F_{\text{net}} = 0\) (no accel.)

Critical Point: Zero net force does not necessarily mean zero velocity. An object can move at constant velocity while its net force is strictly zero!

5 Critical-Thinking Questions

Think carefully through these five conceptual challenges to test deep understanding:

  1. If a football is moving and no one touches it, can a force still be acting on it? Explain.
  2. A car moves at a constant velocity. Does this mean there are no forces acting on it?
  3. If two forces act on an object and their net force is zero, must the object be at rest?
  4. Why are friction and normal forces not considered additional fundamental interactions?
  5. Can a force change the shape of an object without changing its motion?
