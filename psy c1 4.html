Branches and Subfields of Psychology

Ten Specialized Domains Studying Human Growth, Personality, Society, Work, Health, Law, and Clinical Care

Think of psychology like a large hospital: just as different medical specialists treat different conditions, psychology comprises ten specialized branches—each focusing on a distinct aspect of human behavior and mental processes.

1 Lifespan, Individual, Social & Workplace Branches (1–5)

1. Developmental Psychology

Studies physical (height, strength), cognitive (thinking, memory, problem-solving), and psychological (emotions, personality, behavior) changes across the entire lifespan—e.g., how memory evolves from childhood through adulthood.

2. Personality Psychology

Studies relatively enduring individual traits and differences (e.g., self-concept, aggression, moral development)—explaining why one student speaks confidently in public while another is shy.

3. Social Psychology

Examines social interactions, relationships, social perception, and attitudes—such as a student growing to enjoy an activity because their friends participate.

4. Cross-Cultural Psychology

Compares behavior, thought, and emotion across cultures to determine what is universal vs. culture-specific—e.g., comparing how different cultures express respect toward elders.

5. Industrial Psychology

Applies psychology in industries and organizations to improve workplace conditions and boost employee productivity.

2 Legal, Educational, Health & Care Branches (6–10)

6. Forensic Psychology

Applies psychological principles to improve the legal system (police work, testimony, witness reliability evaluation).

7. Educational Psychology

Applies psychological theories to improve the educational process—covering curriculum, teaching methods, and academic program administration.

8. Health Psychology

Applies psychology to the prevention and treatment of physical illness and diseases—helping patients build healthy habits and manage stress.

9. Clinical Psychology

Focuses on the prevention, diagnosis, and treatment of serious psychological disorders—assessing severe difficulties and developing treatment plans.

10. Counseling Psychology

Shares clinical psychology's concern but helps individuals with less severe everyday problems—such as exam stress, relationship issues, or university adjustment.
