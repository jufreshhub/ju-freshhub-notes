Using the Four Tenses in Writing

Combining Present Simple, Past Simple, Present Perfect, and Past Perfect to Write Clear Paragraphs About Personal Experiences

1 Understand the Writing Task

Imagine someone asks you:

"Tell me about your first day at university and your experiences so far."

To answer naturally and clearly in one cohesive paragraph, you need to cover four time frames—each requiring a different tense:

  • Your usual university life → Present Simple
  • What happened on your first day → Past Simple
  • What experiences you have had since joining → Present Perfect
  • What you had done before arriving at university → Past Perfect

2 How to Use the Four Tenses in Your Paragraph

A. Present Simple — Describe Your Current Routine

Use it to explain what normally happens in your university life today.

  • I attend lectures every day.
  • I study English in the evening.
  • My classes start at 8:00 a.m.
B. Past Simple — Describe Your First Day

Use it for completed events that happened on that specific day.

  • I arrived at university early.
  • I met many new students.
  • I attended my first lecture.
  • I felt nervous but excited.
C. Present Perfect — Describe Experiences Up to Now

Use it to describe experiences since you joined university (connecting your past to the present).

  • I have made many friends.
  • I have learned new things.
  • I have visited different places on campus.
  • I have improved my English.
D. Past Perfect — Describe What Happened Before Arriving

Use it for things you completed before another past event (like arriving at university).

  • I had prepared my documents before I arrived.
  • I had never lived away from my family before I joined university.
  • I had studied English before my first lecture.

3 Sample Paragraph: "My First Day at University"

Past Perfect Past Simple Present Perfect Present Simple
My First Day at University

Before I joined university, I had studied hard for my entrance examination. On my first day, I arrived at the campus early and met many new students. I felt excited because everything was new to me. Since joining university, I have made many friends and have learned many interesting things. I usually attend lectures in the morning and study in the library in the afternoon. University life is challenging, but I enjoy it.

Verb / PhraseTenseReason
had studiedPast PerfectHappened before joining university
arrived, met, feltPast SimpleCompleted first-day events
have made, have learnedPresent PerfectExperiences since joining up to now
attend, study, is, enjoyPresent SimpleCurrent routines and general facts

Important: A good paragraph does not force all four tenses into every sentence. Use each tense naturally where its meaning fits best.

4 Common Mistakes When Writing

❌ I have joined university last year.
✅ I joined university last year.

Why? "Last year" is a finished past time → use Past Simple.

❌ She have made many friends.
✅ She has made many friends.

Why? Always use has with she, he, and it.

❌ Before I arrived, the class started.
✅ Before I arrived, the class had started.

Why? The class started earlier than I arrived → Past Perfect.

❌ He study English every day.
✅ He studies English every day.

Why? Present Simple adds -s / -ies with he, she, it.

5 A Simple 4-Step Paragraph Writing Formula

1. Beginning · Before University What had you done before joining?

Use Past Perfect (e.g., I had prepared...).

2. Middle · Your First Day What happened when you arrived?

Use Past Simple (e.g., I arrived, met, felt...).

3. Next · Experiences So Far What have you experienced since joining?

Use Present Perfect (e.g., I have made, have learned...).

4. Ending · Current University Life What do you normally do now?

Use Present Simple (e.g., I attend, study, enjoy...).
