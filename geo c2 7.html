Cenozoic Era (70 million years ago–Present)

Highland Uplifting, Trap Series Flood Basalts, Ethiopian Rift Valley Formation, and Quaternary Volcanism & Lakes

1 What Is the Cenozoic Era?

Beginning approximately 70 million years ago and continuing to the present, the Cenozoic Era is commonly called the Age of Mammals because mammals became dominant following dinosaur extinction. For Ethiopia, it shaped our present-day plateaus, Great Rift Valley, volcanoes, hot springs, and lakes.

Five Major Events in Ethiopia:
  • Uplifting of the Arabo-Ethiopian landmass
  • Formation of extensive volcanic rocks (Flood basalt & Trap Series)
  • Development of the Ethiopian Rift Valley
  • Quaternary volcanic activity
  • Formation of lakes and accumulation of sediments

2 Highland Uplift, Flood Basalt & Trap Series Lava

A. Uplifting of Arabo-Ethiopian Landmass:

Upward crustal rise—like raising bread dough from underneath—elevating highlands, creating steep escarpments, and intensifying river erosion.

B. Flood Basalt & Trap Series:

Enormous basaltic lava flows erupted repeatedly through fissures, forming thick step-like plateau layers called the Trap Series (trap = staircase).

3 Formation of the Ethiopian Rift Valley

Part of the East African Rift System, the Ethiopian Rift Valley formed when tensional forces stretched and thinned the crust—much like gently pulling a piece of chocolate from opposite sides until cracks form and central blocks sink downward.

Key Tectonic Terms & Significance:
  • Tension / Normal Fault: Pulling force creating fractures where the hanging wall moves down.
  • Graben vs. Horst: A graben is the block that sinks between faults; a horst remains higher.
  • Separates the Northwestern and Southeastern Ethiopian Highlands and hosts Rift lakes & geothermal sites.

4 Quaternary Volcanism, Deposition & Lake Formation

Beginning ~2.6 million years ago and continuing today, the Quaternary Period produced volcanic cones, calderas, lava fields, hot springs, and active volcanoes like Erta Ale in Afar.

Quaternary Deposition & Rift Valley Lakes:

Tectonic basins combined with drainage and climate shifts formed major lakes including Lake Ziway, Lake Abijatta, Lake Shala, and Lake Hawassa.
