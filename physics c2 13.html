Linear Momentum

Momentum Vector (\(\vec{p} = m\vec{v}\)), Impulse–Momentum Theorem, Impact Safety, and Momentum Conservation

1 What is Linear Momentum?

Compare stopping a fast tennis ball versus stopping a slowly moving heavy truck. Usually, the truck is much harder to stop because stopping an object depends on both its mass and its velocity.

Definition & Vector Formula:

Linear momentum is the product of an object's mass and velocity:

$$\boxed{\vec{p} = m\vec{v}} \qquad \text{SI Unit:} \quad \boxed{\text{kg}\cdot\text{m/s}}$$
  • \(\vec{p}\) = linear momentum (vector pointing in the exact same direction as velocity)
  • \(m\) = mass in kilograms (\(\text{kg}\))
  • \(\vec{v}\) = velocity in metres per second (\(\text{m/s}\))
Proportional Scaling:
  • \(2\text{ kg}\) at \(5\text{ m/s} \implies 10\text{ kg}\cdot\text{m/s}\)
  • \(4\text{ kg}\) at \(5\text{ m/s} \implies 20\text{ kg}\cdot\text{m/s}\) (double mass → double momentum)
  • \(2\text{ kg}\) at \(10\text{ m/s} \implies 20\text{ kg}\cdot\text{m/s}\) (double velocity → double momentum)
Example 1 — Simple Calculation

A \(5\text{ kg}\) object moves east at \(4\text{ m/s}\):

\(p = mv = 5(4) = \mathbf{20\text{ kg}\cdot\text{m/s east}}\)
Example 2 — Direction & Negative Signs

A \(3\text{ kg}\) object moves west at \(6\text{ m/s}\) (east = \(+\), so \(v = -6\text{ m/s}\)):

\(p = mv = 3(-6) = \mathbf{-18\text{ kg}\cdot\text{m/s}}\) (negative sign denotes westward)
Example 3 — Comparing Two Objects

Object A: \(10\text{ kg}\) at \(3\text{ m/s east}\); Object B: \(5\text{ kg}\) at \(8\text{ m/s east}\):

\(p_A = 10(3) = 30\text{ kg}\cdot\text{m/s}\)  |  \(p_B = 5(8) = 40\text{ kg}\cdot\text{m/s} \implies \mathbf{p_B > p_A}\)

2 Newton's Second Law & Impulse–Momentum Theorem

Momentum Form of Newton's Second Law & Impulse (\(\vec{J}\)):
\(F = ma = m\frac{\Delta v}{\Delta t} = \frac{mv_f - mv_i}{\Delta t} \implies \boxed{\vec{F}_{\text{net}} = \frac{\Delta\vec{p}}{\Delta t}}\)
$$\boxed{\vec{J} = \vec{F}_{\text{net}}\Delta t = \Delta\vec{p} = \vec{p}_f - \vec{p}_i = m(\vec{v}_f - \vec{v}_i)}$$

The SI unit of impulse is \(\text{N}\cdot\text{s}\), which is identical to \(\text{kg}\cdot\text{m/s}\).

Example 4 — A Football Being Kicked

A \(0.5\text{ kg}\) football starts at rest (\(u = 0\)) and is kicked to \(20\text{ m/s east}\):

\(J = m(v - u) = 0.5(20 - 0) = \mathbf{10\text{ N}\cdot\text{s east}}\)
Example 5 — Average Force From Rebounding Ball

A \(0.2\text{ kg}\) ball moves east at \(10\text{ m/s}\), is hit, and rebounds west at \(15\text{ m/s}\) in \(\Delta t = 0.05\text{ s}\).

Step 1 (East = \(+\)): \(u = +10\text{ m/s},\quad v = -15\text{ m/s}\)
Step 2 (Momentum change): \(\Delta p = m(v - u) = 0.2(-15 - 10) = 0.2(-25) = \mathbf{-5\text{ kg}\cdot\text{m/s}}\)
Step 3 (Average force): \(F_{\text{avg}} = \frac{\Delta p}{\Delta t} = \frac{-5}{0.05} = \mathbf{-100\text{ N}}\) (\(100\text{ N west}\))

Common Exam Trap: When direction reverses, final velocity MUST carry a negative sign—do not subtract magnitudes as positive!

Why Do Airbags & Seatbelts Reduce Injuries?

For an identical momentum change \(\Delta p = 100\text{ kg}\cdot\text{m/s}\), increasing the stopping time \(\Delta t\) dramatically reduces the impact force \(F_{\text{avg}} = \frac{\Delta p}{\Delta t}\):

Short stop (\(0.02\text{ s}\)): \(F = \frac{100}{0.02} = 5000\text{ N}\)
Airbag stop (\(0.20\text{ s}\)): \(F = \frac{100}{0.20} = 500\text{ N}\) (10× smaller!)
Critical-Thinking Question

A goalkeeper catches a fast-moving football. Why does the goalkeeper move their hands backward while catching it instead of keeping the hands rigid? Think about stopping time and average force.

3 Conservation of Linear Momentum

Law of Conservation of Linear Momentum:

The total momentum of an isolated system remains constant whenever the net external force acting on the system is zero (arising directly from Newton's Third Law equal-and-opposite internal impulses):

$$\boxed{\vec{p}_{\text{initial}} = \vec{p}_{\text{final}}} \qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad \boxed{m_1\vec{u}_1 + m_2\vec{u}_2 = m_1\vec{v}_1 + m_2\vec{v}_2}$$

Where \(m_1, m_2\) = masses, \(u_1, u_2\) = initial velocities, and \(v_1, v_2\) = final velocities.

Example 6 — Two Skaters Pushing Apart

Skater A (\(60\text{ kg}\)) and Skater B (\(40\text{ kg}\)) stand still on frictionless ice. After pushing off, A moves west at \(2\text{ m/s}\). Find B's velocity.

Step 1: Initially at rest \(\implies p_i = 0\)
Step 2 (East = \(+\)): \(0 = 60(-2) + 40v_B \implies 0 = -120 + 40v_B\)
Step 3: \(40v_B = 120 \implies \mathbf{v_B = +3\text{ m/s}}\) (\(3\text{ m/s east}\); lighter skater moves faster!)
Example 7 — Archer & Arrow Recoil

A \(60\text{ kg}\) archer at rest on ice fires a \(0.50\text{ kg}\) arrow horizontally at \(50\text{ m/s}\). Find the archer's recoil velocity.

Step 1: \(p_i = 0 \implies m_a v_a + m_{\text{arrow}}v_{\text{arrow}} = 0\)
Step 2: \(60v_a + 0.50(50) = 0 \implies 60v_a = -25 \implies \mathbf{v_a = -0.417\text{ m/s}}\) (opposite arrow)
Example 8 — One Object Initially at Rest

A \(2\text{ kg}\) object at \(6\text{ m/s}\) hits a stationary \(4\text{ kg}\) object. Afterwards, the \(2\text{ kg}\) mass moves at \(2\text{ m/s}\) in the same direction. Find \(v_2\).

Step 1: \(p_i = 2(6) + 4(0) = 12\)  |  \(p_f = 2(2) + 4v_2 = 4 + 4v_2\)
Step 2: \(12 = 4 + 4v_2 \implies 8 = 4v_2 \implies \mathbf{v_2 = 2\text{ m/s}}\)
Special Case: Explosions

When a stationary firework explodes into two fragments: \(p_i = 0 \implies m_1 v_1 + m_2 v_2 = 0\). Fragments fly in opposite directions so total momentum remains zero.

4 Important Exam Distinctions & Formulas

Quantity / LawFormulaSI Unit
Momentum\(\vec{p} = m\vec{v}\)kg·m/s
Impulse\(\vec{J} = \vec{F}_{\text{net}}\Delta t\)N·s
Impulse–momentum theorem\(\vec{J} = \Delta\vec{p} = m(\vec{v}_f - \vec{v}_i)\)N·s
Net force\(\vec{F}_{\text{net}} = \frac{\Delta\vec{p}}{\Delta t}\)N
Momentum conservation\(m_1\vec{u}_1 + m_2\vec{u}_2 = m_1\vec{v}_1 + m_2\vec{v}_2\)kg·m/s
  • Both momentum and impulse are vector quantities.
  • Momentum conservation holds for isolated systems (\(\vec{F}_{\text{ext, net}} = 0\)) even if kinetic energy is not conserved!
