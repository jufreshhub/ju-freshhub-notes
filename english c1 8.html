Denotative and Connotative Meanings

Literal Dictionary Definitions, Emotional Associations (Positive, Negative & Neutral), and Choosing Appropriate Words

1 First, Understand the Big Idea

English words can carry two different kinds of meaning. Imagine someone calls a person a "snake". What comes to your mind?

  • Are they talking about an actual legless animal?
  • Or are they saying the person is dishonest and untrustworthy?

The word snake has a literal dictionary meaning, but it also communicates a strong feeling or idea. This is the difference between denotation and connotation.

2 Denotative Meaning — The Dictionary Meaning

Definition:

Denotative meaning is the basic, literal, or dictionary definition of a word—what the word directly names.

WordDenotative (Literal) Meaning
SnakeA long, legless reptile
MotherA female parent
MonkeyAn animal belonging to the primate group
SkinnyVery thin
AdolescentA young person developing from a child into an adult

Easy Memory Trick: Denotation = Dictionary definition (literal truth).

3 Connotative Meaning — The Feeling Behind a Word

Definition:

Connotative meaning refers to the emotions, feelings, and cultural associations connected to a word—what the word makes people feel or think.

Examples of Associations:
  • Mother → love, care, warmth, protection
  • Snake → danger, betrayal, dishonesty
  • Childish → immature, behaving poorly
  • Unique → special, admirable, exceptional

Note: Connotation can shift depending on the situation, culture, and tone of the speaker.

4 Three Types of Connotation

A. Positive Connotation

Creates a pleasant, admiring, or favourable feeling.

  • Unique → special or exceptional
  • Economical → careful and smart with money/resources
  • Elated → extremely happy
  • Mother → love and care
B. Negative Connotation

Creates an unpleasant, critical, or unfavourable feeling.

  • Childish → immature
  • Skinny → unhealthy-looking or unattractively thin
  • Clique → an exclusive group that shuts others out
  • Snake → a dishonest or treacherous person
C. Neutral Connotation

Communicates plain information without a strong positive or negative feeling.

  • Group → a collection of people
  • Adolescent → a person in the teenage stage of development
  • Questioning → asking questions

5 Key Comparison: Group, Clique, and Club

All three words refer to more than one person together, yet the emotional impression they create is very different:

WordDenotative MeaningConnotation
GroupPeople togetherNeutral
CliqueA small group of peopleNegative (exclusive)
ClubPeople organized around a shared interestPositive / Neutral
Compare Three Sentences:
  • • "I joined a study group." → Sounds plain and neutral.
  • • "I joined a clique." → Suggests an unfriendly, exclusive circle.
  • • "I joined a science club." → Suggests an organized, positive shared interest.

7 Tricky Examples & Quick Exam Tips

1. "The snake is hiding under the rock."
Show Answer
✅ Denotative meaning — Here, snake refers literally to an actual animal.
2. "Don't trust him; he is a snake."
Show Answer
✅ Connotative meaning (Negative) — Here, snake metaphorically describes a dishonest person.
3. "She is very economical."
Show Answer
✅ Positive connotation — Suggests she manages money and resources wisely (unlike negative words like stingy or cheap).
4. "He is childish."
Show Answer
✅ Negative connotation — Suggests he behaves immaturely (whereas childlike is usually positive).
5. "The adolescent joined the school club."
Show Answer
✅ Denotative / Neutral — Simply identifies the person's age group without emotional judgment.
Quick Exam Guide:
If the question asks about…Think of…
Dictionary definition / Literal meaningDenotation
Feelings associated with a wordConnotation
Pleasant / favourable impressionPositive connotation
Unpleasant / critical impressionNegative connotation
No strong emotional impressionNeutral connotation
Important Warning: Do not confuse denotation/connotation with positive/negative. Denotation is the literal dictionary meaning; connotation is the emotional association (which can be positive, negative, or neutral).
