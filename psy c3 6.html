Applications of Learning Theories

Classroom Practice, Behavioral Reinforcement Techniques, and Effective University Study Strategies

1 Applying Classical & Operant Conditioning

1. Classical Conditioning (Learned Emotional Responses):

If a student suffers repeated embarrassment and harsh criticism in mathematics class, the subject or room itself becomes associated with anxiety—making the student anxious before the lesson even starts. Therefore, teachers must build pleasant classroom atmospheres, encourage warm participation, and reduce learned fears gradually via desensitization.

2. Operant Conditioning (Six Practical Classroom Uses):
  • A. Reinforcing desirable behavior: Use prizes, medals, smiles, praise, encouragement, affectionate patting on the back, and higher marks (e.g., praising homework completion or discussion).
  • B. Managing problem behavior: Prioritize reinforcing cooperation and appropriate participation rather than relying mainly on punishment.
  • C. Dealing with anxiety: Introduce feared stimuli weakly at first and increase gradually to reduce anxiety.
  • D. Conditioning group behavior: Reward relevant class questions to raise whole-class participation.
  • E. Supporting cognitive learning: Break material into small logical steps with immediate feedback/reinforcement at each step.
  • F. Shaping complex skills: Teach a lab procedure step by step—arrange equipment → prepare materials → execute each step → combine into the full procedure.

2 Applying Social Learning & Cognitive Theories

3. Social Learning Theory
  • Positive modeling: Teachers/parents arrive on time, respect students, and speak politely.
  • Vicarious learning: Seeing a classmate praised for answering a hard question motivates observers.
  • Diverse role models: Challenge stereotypes; build self-efficacy, realistic goals, and self-regulation.
4. Cognitive Learning Theory
  • Understand vs. memorize: Understand how blood flows through heart chambers and valves—not just chamber names.
  • Meaningful physics: Explain what each formula variable represents and apply it to real life.
  • Logical connections: Link new topics to prior knowledge and invite independent problem solving.
