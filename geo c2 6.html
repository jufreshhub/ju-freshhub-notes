Mesozoic Era (225–70 million years ago)

Epeirogenesis, Marine Transgression & Regression, Sedimentary Stratigraphy, and Hydrocarbon Potential

1 What Was the Mesozoic Era?

Lasting approximately 225–70 million years ago, the Mesozoic Era is commonly known as the Age of Reptiles because reptiles and dinosaurs dominated terrestrial ecosystems. For Ethiopia, however, the defining events were crustal uplift and sinking, ancient marine invasions, and major sedimentary rock formation.

2 Epeirogenesis, Marine Transgression & Regression

Epeirogenesis (slow, broad uplift or sinking of large crustal blocks, like tilting a wooden table) altered the ancient Arabo-Ethiopian landmass relative to sea level, causing alternating sea advances and retreats:

A. Marine Transgression:

Sea advances over land as sea level rises or land slowly sinks (like a rising tide covering a beach).

B. Marine Regression:

Sea retreats from land as sea level falls or land slowly rises (like the tide going out, exposing dry land).

3 Major Mesozoic Sedimentary Rocks in Ethiopia

1. Adigrat Sandstone (Oldest / Lower Formation)

Consists mainly of compacted sandstone formed in continental and coastal depositional environments.

2. Hintalo (Antalo) Limestone (Middle Formation)

Lies above Adigrat Sandstone; formed in marine environments from calcium carbonate during sea transgression.

3. Upper Sandstone (Youngest / Top Formation)

Lies above Hintalo Limestone; deposited as marine conditions changed and the sea retreated (marine regression).

Simplified Stratigraphic Sequence (Youngest on Top → Oldest at Bottom):
3. Upper Sandstone (Youngest)
2. Hintalo (Antalo) Limestone (Middle)
1. Adigrat Sandstone (Oldest)

4 Distribution & Petroleum Potential

Distribution in Ethiopia:

Exposed primarily in Northern Ethiopia (Tigray), Eastern Ethiopia, and Southeastern lowlands (Ogaden region).

Oil & Natural Gas Potential (Ogaden Basin):

Sedimentary basins store hydrocarbons via Source rock (organic matter), Reservoir rock (porous sandstone), and Trap structures.
