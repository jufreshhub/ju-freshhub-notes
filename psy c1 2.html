Goals of Psychology

Description, Explanation, Prediction, and Behavioral & Experimental Control

Why do psychologists study behavior and mental processes, and what are they trying to achieve? Psychology pursues four interconnected goals—functioning like four logical steps used to understand and guide human behavior:

1. Description What is happening?
2. Explanation Why is it happening?
3. Prediction What will happen?
4. Control How to change it?

1 Description — What Is Happening?

Description means observing behavior and carefully noting what is occurring. Psychologists ask: What is happening? Where does it happen? To whom does it happen? Under what circumstances?

Classroom Example:

A teacher notices a student rarely does homework, earns lower grades, and displays a negative attitude toward the course. The teacher is simply observing and recording—without yet determining why.

Medical Analogy:

Before diagnosing an illness, a doctor first observes and records a patient's symptoms. Recording symptoms without explaining the cause is description.

2 Explanation — Why Is It Happening?

Explanation means searching for the underlying reasons behind observed behavior. Identifying causes helps psychologists formulate a theory—a general explanation of a set of observations or facts.

Continuing the Student Example:

To understand why the girl avoids homework and performs poorly, the teacher investigates her home background and peers—discovering she receives attention from others when she behaves oddly.

Everyday Phone Analogy:

Description notes that a phone is not charging; explanation investigates why—such as a damaged cable or faulty charging port.

3 Prediction — What Will Happen in the Future?

Prediction means estimating what will likely happen in the future based on previous research into similar situations.

Continuing the Student Example:

Drawing on past studies, a counselor predicts that if the girl's pattern continues unchanged, she will likely fail to reach her full learning potential.

Weather Analogy:

Observing dark clouds gathering and atmospheric conditions leads you to predict that rain will soon fall.

4 Control — How Can Behavior Be Changed?

A. Behavioral Modification:

Changing behavior from an undesirable condition (failing in school) to a desirable condition (achieving academic success) using targeted learning strategies.

B. Experimental Control in Research:

Accounting for extraneous factors when studying variables. For example, when examining intelligence vs. academic performance among freshmen, researchers must control for family socioeconomic status.

5 Unified Example & Exam Distinctions

One Situation, Four Goals (Freshman Missing Classes Frequently):
  • • Description (What?): Noting that the student misses classes frequently.
  • • Explanation (Why?): Investigating possible causes, such as difficulties at home.
  • • Prediction (What next?): Estimating future academic decline if absences continue.
  • • Control (How to change it?): Applying intervention strategies to improve attendance and success.

DescriptionWhat?Observe and record behavior
ExplanationWhy?Find underlying causes & build theories

Common Exam Trap: Do not confuse explanation (finding the reason for current behavior) with prediction (estimating future consequences) or control (modifying behavior).
