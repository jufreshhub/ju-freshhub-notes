Conditional Sentences

First, Second & Third Conditionals, Alternative Conditional Expressions, Mixed Conditionals, and Exam Mistakes

1 What is a Conditional Sentence?

Question: "What will happen if you study hard?"

Answer: "If I study hard, I will pass my examination."

A conditional sentence expresses a condition and what happens as a result of that condition.

Conditionals describe three key situations:

  • What will you do if you meet someone? (Real future)
  • What would you do if you met someone? (Imaginary present/future)
  • What would you have done if you had met someone in the past? (Imaginary past)
The Two Parts of a Conditional Sentence
If-Clause (Condition) If I study hard,
Main Clause (Result) I will pass.

Changing Clause Order: Both orders are grammatically correct:

  • If I study hard, I will pass. (Use a comma when the if-clause comes first.)
  • I will pass if I study hard. (No comma needed when the main clause comes first.)

2 First Conditional — Real Possibility (Possible Future)

We use the first conditional to talk about something that is genuinely possible in the future and its likely result.

Formula
If + Present Simple , will + Base Verb

"If I study hard, I will pass the examination."

  • Condition: I study hard.
  • Likely Result: I will pass.
More Examples:
  • If it rains, I will stay at home.
  • If you exercise regularly, you will improve your fitness.
  • If I meet Derartu Tulu, I will ask her a question.
Important Exam Rule:

After if, always use the Present Simple in this pattern—never will.

✅ Correct: If I study, I will pass.
❌ Incorrect: If I will study, I will pass.

3 Second Conditional — Imaginary or Unlikely Situation

We use the second conditional to imagine a situation that is unreal, unlikely, or not true in the present.

Formula
If + Past Simple , would + Base Verb

"If I were rich, I would build a hospital."

Are you rich right now? No—you are imagining a hypothetical situation.

More Examples:
  • If I met Zinedine Zidane, I would ask for his autograph.
  • If I had more time, I would read more books.
  • If I were the teacher, I would help every student.
Special Expression: "If I were you" (Giving Advice)

In formal grammar, we use were (not was) for all subjects in imaginary situations:

  • If I were you, I would study every day.
  • If I were you, I would not waste time.

Remember: Use would + base verb (e.g., would help), never would to + verb.

4 Third Conditional — Imaginary Past (Unchangeable Past)

We use the third conditional to imagine a different result for something that already happened in the past and cannot now be changed.

Formula
If + had + Past Participle (V3) , would have + Past Participle (V3)

"If I had studied hard, I would have passed the examination."

  • Reality: I did not study hard, and I did not pass.
  • Imaginary Past: If I had studied, I would have passed.
More Examples:
  • If I had woken up early, I would have caught the bus.
  • If she had practiced, she would have won the competition.
  • If I had met Derartu Tulu after her victory in 1992, I would have congratulated her.

5 Comparing the Three Conditional Types

TypeIf-ClauseMain ClauseMeaning
FirstPresent simplewill + base verbPossible future
SecondPast simplewould + base verbImaginary / unlikely
Thirdhad + past participlewould have + past participleImaginary past
Comparing One Situation Across All Three Types:
First Conditional

"If I meet a famous footballer, I will take a photo."

It is genuinely possible that I will meet one.

Second Conditional

"If I met a famous footballer, I would take a photo."

I am imagining meeting one right now / in an unlikely scenario.

Third Conditional

"If I had met a famous footballer, I would have taken a photo."

I did not meet one in the past, and I am imagining what I would have done.

6 Alternative Expressions & Common Exam Mistakes

ExpressionExample Sentence
Provided (that)You can go provided that you finish your homework.
As long asYou can succeed as long as you work hard.
On condition thatYou can go out on condition that you finish your work.
Even ifI would fail even if I studied.
Key Difference in Meaning:
  • Provided that / As long as / On condition that: Introduce a strict requirement or condition (similar to only if).
  • Even if: Shows that the result stays the same regardless of the condition.
Common Examination Mistakes:
❌ Incorrect✅ Correct
If I will study, I will pass.If I study, I will pass.
If I was rich, I would travel.If I were rich, I would travel.
If I had studied, I would pass.If I had studied, I would have passed.
If she had went, she would have seen him.If she had gone, she would have seen him.
If I met him, I will greet him.If I met him, I would greet him.
Three Formulas to Memorize:
  • First: Present Simple + will + V1
  • Second: Past Simple + would + V1
  • Third: had + V3 + would have + V3

7 Quick Practice — Identify the Conditional Type

1. "If I exercise, I will become healthier."
Show Answer
✅ First Conditional (Present simple exercise + will become → real future possibility).
2. "If I were a doctor, I would help poor people."
Show Answer
✅ Second Conditional (Past simple were + would help → imaginary present situation).
3. "If I had studied, I would have passed."
Show Answer
✅ Third Conditional (had studied + would have passed → imaginary unchangeable past).
Tricky 4. "If you had done your homework, you would be allowed to go out now."
Show Answer
✅ Mixed Conditional — Connects a past condition (had done → 3rd conditional) with a present result (would be allowed... now → 2nd conditional).
