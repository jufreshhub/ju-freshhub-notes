Factors Influencing Learning

Motivation, Maturation, Physical Health, Psychological Wellbeing, Environment, Background, Time, and Spacing

1 Internal Drives & Readiness: Motivation and Maturation

1. Motivation (Fuel of a Car)

Internal drive or desire encouraging learning. Stronger, clearer motives produce greater enthusiasm and effort (e.g., studying medicine out of genuine passion to heal vs. family pressure).

2. Maturation (Neuromuscular Coordination)

Natural biological and nervous-system development preparing a learner (e.g., a 6-month-old baby cannot walk until muscles mature). Maturation = readiness; Practice = repeated improvement.

2 Physical Health & Psychological Wellbeing

3. Health Condition of the Learner (Low Phone Battery Analogy)

Physical illness or exhaustion weakens attention and memory (like sitting through a 2-hour lecture after staying awake all night). Five physical barriers include:

  • Sensory defects: Hearing or vision problems blocking input.
  • Malnutrition & Toxic conditions: Poor nutrition or harmful substances disrupting brain functioning.
  • Loss of sleep & Fatigue: Sleep deprivation and exhaustion cutting concentration and recall.
Psychological Barriers (Hinder Learning):

Worries (e.g., family crisis), fears, loneliness, and feelings of inferiority (afraid peers will laugh) distract attention.

Psychological Supports (Aid Learning):

Self-confidence (believing in ability), Self-reliance (depending on own judgment), and Self-respect (positive self-regard).

3 Environment & Prior Knowledge

5A. Good Working Conditions

Fresh air, adequate light, comfortable surroundings, moderate temperature, learning aids, minimal distractions (quiet library).

5B. Poor Working Conditions

Poor ventilation, dim light, uncomfortable seating, extreme temperatures, loud music/noise distractions, lack of aids.

6. Background Experiences (House Foundation Analogy)

Prior knowledge supports new learning: understanding blood, vessels, and heart function prepares you to grasp cardiac pumping; knowing multiplication simplifies learning division.

4 Study Duration & Massed vs. Distributed Practice

Too Long (8 hrs) Fatigue & drop in focus
Too Short (5 min) Inadequate practice
Balanced + Breaks Optimal mastery
8A. Massed Learning (Cramming)

Studying 5 chapters or 100 psychology terms continuously the night before an exam without breaks.

8B. Distributed Learning (Spaced)

Spreading study across days (1 chapter/day or 20 terms/day for 5 days with review)—consistently outperforms cramming!
