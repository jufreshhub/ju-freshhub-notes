Territorial Area, Continental Rankings, Advantages & Disadvantages of Size, Country Shapes, and Compactness Indices

Think of a country like a piece of land on a map. Its size tells us how much land it covers, while its shape tells us how that land is arranged.

3 Part 2: Shape of Ethiopia — Five Major Country Shapes

Countries have different geographical shapes, classified into five main categories based on arrangement:

1. Compact Shape:

Distance from geographical center to borders varies little (circular/square). Advantage: easier defense and integration.

2. Fragmented Shape:

Territory is divided into separate parts separated by water, land, or other countries.

3. Elongated Shape:

Geographically long and relatively narrow (e.g., Chile).

4. Perforated Shape:

Completely surrounds another country (e.g., South Africa surrounding Lesotho).

5. Protruded Shape:

Has one portion much more elongated than the rest (e.g., Myanmar and Eritrea).

4 Part 3: Measuring Compactness & Indices

Compactness describes how closely a country approaches a circular shape. Because no country is perfectly circular, geographers use four mathematical indices:

IndexFormula / RatioGreater Compactness Rule
Area–Boundary (A/B) \(\frac{\text{Area}}{\text{Boundary}}\) Higher value is better
Boundary–Circumference (B/C) \(\frac{\text{Country Boundary}}{\text{Equal-Area Circle Circumference}}\) Closer to 1 is better
Area–Circumference (A/C) \(\frac{\text{Area}}{\text{Smallest Inscribing Circle Circumference}}\) Higher value is better
Area–Area (A/A′) \(\frac{\text{Actual Area}}{\text{Smallest Inscribing Circle Area}}\) Closer to 1 is better
Compactness Calculation Worked Examples:
Example 1 — Area-Boundary Ratio (A/B)

Calculate the A/B index for a country with a total area of \(1,106,000\text{ km}^2\) and a total boundary length of \(5,260\text{ km}\).

\(\text{A/B} = \frac{\text{Area}}{\text{Boundary}} = \frac{1,106,000}{5,260} \approx \mathbf{210.27}\)
Example 2 — Boundary-Circumference Ratio (B/C)

If a country's actual boundary length is \(4,800\text{ km}\) and the circumference of its equal-area circle is \(3,400\text{ km}\), compute its B/C index.

\(\text{B/C} = \frac{\text{Country Boundary}}{\text{Equal-Area Circle Circumference}} = \frac{4,800}{3,400} \approx \mathbf{1.41}\)

Note: Since \(1.41\) is close to \(1\), this indicates good circular compactness.

Example 3 — Area-Circumference Ratio (A/C)

Find the A/C index for a country with an area of \(900,000\text{ km}^2\) and a smallest inscribing circle circumference of \(3,034\text{ km}\).

\(\text{A/C} = \frac{\text{Area}}{\text{Inscribing Circle Circumference}} = \frac{900,000}{3,034} \approx \mathbf{296.61}\)
Example 4 — Comparing Two Countries (A/B Index)

Country X has Area = \(500,000\text{ km}^2\) & Boundary = \(4,000\text{ km}\). Country Y has Area = \(800,000\text{ km}^2\) & Boundary = \(7,000\text{ km}\). Which is more compact?

\(\text{Country X: } \text{A/B} = \frac{500,000}{4,000} = \mathbf{125}\)
\(\text{Country Y: } \text{A/B} = \frac{800,000}{7,000} \approx \mathbf{114.28}\)
Conclusion: Country X is more compact because it has a higher A/B ratio (\(125 > 114.28\)).
