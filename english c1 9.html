Collocations

Natural Verb–Noun Combinations, Six Essential Collocations, Common Expressions, and Memory Techniques

1 What is a Collocation?

Definition:

A collocation is a combination of words that naturally go together in English.

Think of collocations like friendship: certain words are commonly paired together, while other combinations sound unnatural to fluent English speakers—even if individual words have similar meanings.

✅ We naturally say: make a noise
❌ We normally don't say: do a noise

2 Verb Collocations

A verb collocation is a combination of a verb + another word (usually a noun) that naturally belongs together. Always learn the whole combination, not just the individual words:

Make + noise
Do + business
Take + break
Pay + attention

3 Six Important Collocations

Let's understand each one (tap any thumbnail to enlarge):

1. Make a noise

Meaning: Produce a sound.

Example: The children made a noise in the classroom.

2. Do business

Meaning: Carry out commercial or business activities.

Example: Many companies do business internationally.

3. Take a break

Meaning: Stop working or studying for a short time to rest.

Example: I take a break after two hours of studying.

4. Pay attention

Meaning: Listen or watch carefully.

Example: Please pay attention to the teacher.

5. Keep a promise

Meaning: Do what you promised to do.

Example: A trustworthy person keeps a promise.

6. Catch a disease

Meaning: Become infected with a disease.

Example: People can catch a disease through exposure to infection.

How to Remember Collocations:
  • Learn the verb and noun together as one unit.
  • Create one personal example sentence for each pair.
  • Practise recalling the missing word: "Please pay ______ to the lesson." → pay attention.
Exam Tip: When completing a collocation, choose the verb that naturally combines with the given noun—not simply any verb with a similar dictionary meaning.
