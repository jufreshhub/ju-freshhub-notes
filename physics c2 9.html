Newton's Law of Universal Gravitation

Inverse-Square Law, Mutual Gravitational Attraction, Derivation of \(g\), and Altitude Variation

1 What is Universal Gravitation?

Why does an apple fall toward Earth? Why does the Moon orbit Earth instead of flying away? Why does Earth revolve around the Sun? The answer to all these phenomena is gravitational force.

Law Statement:

Every particle in the universe attracts every other particle with a gravitational force. Earth attracts the Moon, the Moon attracts Earth, Earth attracts you, and you also attract Earth!

Always Attractive:

Unlike magnetic or electrostatic forces, ordinary gravitational interactions between masses never repel.

Why Called Universal?

Gravity acts between all objects possessing mass—even two students standing nearby attract each other, though the force is tiny because their masses are small.

2 Newton's Universal Gravitation Equation

$$\boxed{F = G\frac{m_1 m_2}{r^2}}$$
$$\boxed{G = 6.674 \times 10^{-11}\text{ N}\cdot\text{m}^2/\text{kg}^2}$$
SymbolMeaningSI Unit
\(F\)Gravitational force magnitudeN
\(G\)Universal gravitational constantN·m²/kg²
\(m_1\)First masskg
\(m_2\)Second masskg
\(r\)Distance between centers of massesm
Important: \(r\) is strictly the distance between the centers of the two objects, not the gap between their outer surfaces.

3 Proportional & Inverse-Square Relationships

A. Relationship With Mass (\(F \propto m_1 m_2\))

Doubling one mass doubles \(F\). Doubling both masses yields \(F' = G\frac{(2m_1)(2m_2)}{r^2} = \mathbf{4F}\).

B. Inverse-Square Distance (\(F \propto \frac{1}{r^2}\))

If two planets move from \(100\text{ million km}\) apart to \(200\text{ million km}\), force drops to one-quarter, not half, because distance is squared!

Change in Center Distance (\(r'\))New Gravitational Force (\(F'\))
\(2r\) (Doubled)\(\frac{1}{4}F\)
\(3r\) (Tripled)\(\frac{1}{9}F\)
\(4r\) (Quadrupled)\(\frac{1}{16}F\)
\(\frac{1}{2}r\) (Halved)\(4F\)
\(\frac{1}{3}r\) (One-third)\(9F\)
\(n\,r\) (Scaled by \(n\))\(\frac{1}{n^2}F\)

4 Worked Examples — Universal Gravitation

Example 1 — Basic Gravitational Force Calculation

Two objects have masses \(5\text{ kg}\) and \(10\text{ kg}\) with centers separated by \(2\text{ m}\). Calculate the gravitational force (\(G = 6.674 \times 10^{-11}\)).

Step 1: List given values and write the universal law
\(m_1 = 5\text{ kg},\; m_2 = 10\text{ kg},\; r = 2\text{ m},\; G = 6.674 \times 10^{-11}\text{ N}\cdot\text{m}^2/\text{kg}^2\)
\(F = G\frac{m_1 m_2}{r^2}\)
Step 2: Substitute numbers and simplify
\(F = \frac{(6.674 \times 10^{-11})(5)(10)}{2^2} = \frac{3.337 \times 10^{-9}}{4}\)
\(F = \mathbf{8.3425 \times 10^{-10}\text{ N}}\) (extremely small!)
Example 2 — Force When Distance Doubles

Two masses attract each other with \(F = 80\text{ N}\). What will the force become if their separation doubles (\(r' = 2r\))?

Step 1: Apply inverse-square ratio without needing \(m_1, m_2, G\)
\(F' = F\left(\frac{r}{r'}\right)^2 = 80\left(\frac{r}{2r}\right)^2\)
Step 2: Evaluate square
\(F' = 80\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^2 = 80\left(\frac{1}{4}\right) = \mathbf{20\text{ N}}\)
Example 3 — Changing Both Masses and Distance

Initial force is \(F = 12\text{ N}\). The first mass triples, the second mass doubles, and separation doubles. Find \(F'\).

Step 1: Identify scaling factors
\(m_1' = 3m_1,\quad m_2' = 2m_2,\quad r' = 2r\)
Step 2: Compute proportional ratio \(\frac{F'}{F}\)
\(\frac{F'}{F} = \frac{(3)(2)}{(2)^2} = \frac{6}{4} = 1.5\)
Step 3: Multiply by original force
\(F' = 1.5 \times 12 = \mathbf{18\text{ N}}\)

5 Newton's Third Law & Deriving Acceleration Due to Gravity (\(g\))

Equal Forces, Unequal Accelerations:

If Earth pulls an apple down with \(10\text{ N}\), the apple pulls Earth up with \(10\text{ N}\): \(F_{\text{Earth on apple}} = F_{\text{apple on Earth}}\). Earth doesn't visibly accelerate toward the apple because Earth's mass is enormous (\(a = \frac{F}{m}\)).

Deriving \(g\) Near Earth's Surface:

Set weight \(W = mg\) equal to Newton's gravitational attraction \(F = G\frac{M_E m}{r^2}\):

\(m g = G\frac{M_E m}{r^2}\)
Cancel object mass \(m\) from both sides: \(\boxed{g = \frac{GM_E}{r^2}}\)
At Earth's surface (\(r \approx R_E\)): \(\boxed{g = \frac{GM_E}{R_E^2} \approx 9.8\text{ m/s}^2}\)
Important Distinction: Gravitational force depends on the object's mass, whereas gravitational acceleration \(g\) at a fixed distance from Earth's center does not depend on the object's mass.
Worked Example 4 — Calculating Earth's Surface Gravity \(g\)

\(G = 6.67 \times 10^{-11},\quad M_E = 5.98 \times 10^{24}\text{ kg},\quad R_E = 6.38 \times 10^6\text{ m}\)

Step 1: Write formula: \(g = \frac{GM_E}{R_E^2}\)
Step 2: Substitute: \(g = \frac{(6.67 \times 10^{-11})(5.98 \times 10^{24})}{(6.38 \times 10^6)^2}\)
Step 3: Evaluate: \(g \approx \mathbf{9.80\text{ m/s}^2}\)

6 Gravity at Altitude (\(h\)) & \(G\) vs. \(g\)

At a height \(h\) above Earth's surface, the distance from Earth's center is \(r = R_E + h\):

$$\boxed{g_h = \frac{GM_E}{(R_E + h)^2}} \qquad \text{or} \qquad \boxed{g_h = g_0\left(\frac{R_E}{R_E + h}\right)^2}$$
Worked Example 5 — Gravity at Altitude \(h = R_E\)

An object is at a height equal to Earth's radius above Earth's surface (\(g_0 = 9.8\text{ m/s}^2\)). Calculate \(g_h\).

Step 1: Find total center-to-object distance \(r\)
Since \(h = R_E \implies r = R_E + h = R_E + R_E = \mathbf{2R_E}\)
Step 2: Apply the inverse-square ratio
\(g_h = g_0\left(\frac{R_E}{2R_E}\right)^2 = 9.8\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^2\)
\(g_h = 9.8(0.25) = \mathbf{2.45\text{ m/s}^2}\)

Exam Trap: Never plug \(h\) alone into the denominator—always use \(r = R_E + h\) (distance from Earth's center).

7 Critical-Thinking Questions

  1. If Earth and the Moon attract each other with equal gravitational forces, why does the Moon move much more noticeably than Earth?
  2. If the mass of an object doubles, what happens to its weight? What happens to its gravitational acceleration at the same location?
  3. Why does gravitational force become one-quarter when the distance between two objects doubles?
  4. If an astronaut travels far away from Earth, does gravity completely disappear?
  5. Why do two objects of different masses fall with the same acceleration in a vacuum?
  6. If the gravitational force between two objects is \(100\text{ N}\), what happens if both masses double and their separation also doubles?
