Collisions

Elastic, Inelastic & Perfectly Inelastic Impacts, Relative Speed Shortcuts, and Kinetic Energy Losses

1 What is a Collision?

A collision is an interaction between two or more objects during a relatively short time interval in which they exert strong internal forces on each other (e.g., billiard balls hitting, a bullet entering a wooden block, car crashes, or a hammer striking a nail).

Momentum Conservation in Collisions:

Provided the system is isolated (net external impulse is zero), total momentum is always conserved in all collision types:

$$\boxed{p_{\text{before}} = p_{\text{after}}} \qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad \boxed{m_1 u_1 + m_2 u_2 = m_1 v_1 + m_2 v_2}$$

2 The Three Types of Collisions

Collisions are categorized based on whether kinetic energy is conserved:

A. Elastic Collision

Both momentum and total kinetic energy are conserved. Objects separate after impact (e.g., idealized billiard balls, atomic particles).

B. Inelastic Collision

Momentum is conserved, but kinetic energy is NOT conserved (converted to heat, sound, or permanent deformation, e.g., car crashes).

C. Perfectly Inelastic Collision

Objects stick together after impact, moving with a single common final velocity (e.g., bullet embedding in a wooden block).

Exam Trap: Every perfectly inelastic collision is inelastic, but not every inelastic collision is perfectly inelastic.

3 One-Dimensional Elastic Collisions

Equations & Relative Speed Shortcut:
Momentum: \(m_1 u_1 + m_2 u_2 = m_1 v_1 + m_2 v_2\)
KE: \(\frac{1}{2}m_1 u_1^2 + \frac{1}{2}m_2 u_2^2 = \frac{1}{2}m_1 v_1^2 + \frac{1}{2}m_2 v_2^2\)
Relative Speed Shortcut: \(\boxed{u_1 - u_2 = v_2 - v_1}\) (Approach speed = Separation speed)
Example 1 — Two Equal-Mass Billiard Balls

Ball 1 (\(2\text{ kg}\)) moves right at \(6\text{ m/s}\) and hits an identical stationary Ball 2 (\(2\text{ kg}\)) elastically.

For equal masses in 1D elastic collision, velocities exchange: \(\mathbf{v_1 = 0}\) and \(\mathbf{v_2 = 6\text{ m/s}}\).
Example 2 — Unequal Masses (Elastic)

Block 1 (\(1.6\text{ kg}\), \(u_1 = +4\text{ m/s}\)) collides elastically with Block 2 (\(2.1\text{ kg}\), \(u_2 = -2.5\text{ m/s}\)). Find \(v_1\) and \(v_2\).

Step 1 (Momentum): \(1.6(4) + 2.1(-2.5) = 1.6v_1 + 2.1v_2 \implies \mathbf{1.6v_1 + 2.1v_2 = 1.15}\text{ --- (1)}\)
Step 2 (Relative speed): \(4 - (-2.5) = v_2 - v_1 \implies \mathbf{v_2 = v_1 + 6.5}\text{ --- (2)}\)
Step 3 (Substitute): \(1.6v_1 + 2.1(v_1 + 6.5) = 1.15 \implies 3.7v_1 = -12.5 \implies \mathbf{v_1 = -3.38\text{ m/s}}\)
Step 4 (Find \(v_2\)): \(v_2 = -3.38 + 6.5 = \mathbf{+3.12\text{ m/s}}\) (both reverse direction)

4 Inelastic Collisions — Arrow Passes Through Target

Example 3 — Arrow Passes Through a Moving Target

An archer shoots a \(22.5\text{ g}\) arrow at \(35.0\text{ m/s}\) toward a \(300\text{ g}\) target sliding toward it at \(2.50\text{ m/s}\). The arrow passes through, bringing the target to rest (\(v_2 = 0\)). Find the arrow's final speed.

Step 1 (Convert masses): \(m_1 = 0.0225\text{ kg},\quad m_2 = 0.300\text{ kg}\)
Step 2 (Velocities): \(u_1 = +35.0\text{ m/s},\quad u_2 = -2.50\text{ m/s},\quad v_2 = 0\)
Step 3 (Momentum): \(0.0225(35) + 0.300(-2.5) = 0.0225v_1 + 0\)
Step 4: \(0.7875 - 0.750 = 0.0225v_1 \implies 0.0375 = 0.0225v_1\)
Step 5: \(v_1 = \mathbf{1.67\text{ m/s}}\) (continues in original direction)

5 Perfectly Inelastic Collisions (Objects Stick Together)

Common Final Velocity Formula:
$$\boxed{v = \frac{m_1 u_1 + m_2 u_2}{m_1 + m_2}}$$
Example 4 — Two Cars Stick Together

Car 1 (\(1000\text{ kg}\), \(20\text{ m/s east}\)) hits Car 2 (\(1500\text{ kg}\), \(10\text{ m/s east}\)).

\(v = \frac{1000(20) + 1500(10)}{1000 + 1500} = \frac{20000 + 15000}{2500} = \mathbf{14\text{ m/s east}}\)
Example 5 — Objects Moving Toward Each Other

Trolley 1 (\(4\text{ kg}\), \(8\text{ m/s right}\)) and Trolley 2 (\(6\text{ kg}\), \(3\text{ m/s left}\)) stick together:

\(v = \frac{4(8) + 6(-3)}{4 + 6} = \frac{32 - 18}{10} = \mathbf{1.4\text{ m/s right}}\)
Example 6 — Bullet Embedded in Wooden Block

A \(10.0\text{ g}\) bullet (\(0.0100\text{ kg}\)) embeds in a \(5.00\text{ kg}\) block. Combined speed is \(0.600\text{ m/s}\).

Step 1: \(0.0100u_b + 5(0) = (5.010)(0.600)\)
Step 2: \(0.0100u_b = 3.006 \implies \mathbf{u_b = 300.6\text{ m/s}}\)

6 Kinetic Energy Lost in Inelastic Collisions

Energy Loss Formula:
$$\boxed{KE_{\text{lost}} = KE_i - KE_f}$$
Example 7 — Kinetic Energy Lost When Sticking

A \(2\text{ kg}\) trolley at \(6\text{ m/s}\) hits a stationary \(4\text{ kg}\) trolley and sticks:

Step 1 (Common velocity): \(v = \frac{2(6) + 4(0)}{2 + 4} = 2\text{ m/s}\)
Step 2 (Initial KE): \(KE_i = \frac{1}{2}(2)(6^2) + 0 = 36\text{ J}\)
Step 3 (Final KE): \(KE_f = \frac{1}{2}(2 + 4)(2^2) = 12\text{ J}\)
Step 4 (Lost KE): \(KE_{\text{lost}} = 36 - 12 = \mathbf{24\text{ J}}\) (transformed into heat/sound)
