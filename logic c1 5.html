Importance of Learning Philosophy

Examining Life (Socrates), Maintenance vs. Actualizing Needs, Five Core Benefits, and Bertrand Russell on Uncertainty

1 Examining Life & Two Types of Human Needs

“The unexamined life is not worth living.” — Socrates

Unlike Student A (who accepts everything passively and conforms blindly), Student B questions beliefs, scrutinizes choices, thinks independently, and seeks self-understanding. Philosophy equips us to examine ourselves and the world critically.

A. Maintenance Needs

Physical and psychological essentials needed to sustain survival as human beings: food, shelter, security, and social interaction.

B. Actualizing Needs

Higher aspirations tied to growth and fulfillment: creativity, self-expression, realizing potential, and self-fulfillment (ideals actively promoted by philosophy).

2 Five Major Benefits of Learning Philosophy (I-R-F-C-V)

1. Intellectual & Behavioral Independence

Developing your own reasoned opinions rather than blindly copying friends' career advice—weighing your own interests, abilities, and goals.

2. Reflective Self-Awareness

Asking: Why do I believe what I believe? Why do I act this way? What are my strengths, weaknesses, and reasons?

3. Flexibility, Tolerance & Open-Mindedness

Listening respectfully to conflicting viewpoints (e.g., debating different economic systems) and examining reasons without prejudice.

4. Creative & Critical Thinking

Analyzing root causes deeply—investigating failing grades via teaching methods, exam difficulty, prep time, and materials rather than assuming “students are lazy.”

5. Well-Thought-Out Value Systems

Evaluating moral, artistic, political, and social values—examining why honesty matters and whether truth should always be told even if it causes hurt.

3 Dealing With Uncertainty (Bertrand Russell) & Revision Matrix

Bertrand Russell — The Problems of Philosophy:

Without philosophy, a person stays trapped in common-sense prejudices, inherited societal beliefs, and unreasoned convictions—assuming custom (“We have always done it this way”) is the only truth. While philosophy may reduce dogmatic certainty, it expands thinking, suggests possibilities, frees us from the tyranny of custom, reduces arrogance, and keeps wonder alive.
